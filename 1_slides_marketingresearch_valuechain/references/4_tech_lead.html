Case: TheRealReal, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: what gets built in-house and what gets bought. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the software. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

What has been built

“Technology powers all aspects of our business, including our complex, individual stock keeping unit (single-SKU) inventory management system. Our supply comes from thousands of individual consignors and businesses across the United States. Given the complexity of our inventory model, we developed AI enabled, specialized, proprietary applications to optimize inbound processes. We increasingly use AI in our technology platform to automate item attribution, authentication, pricing, copywriting and photo retouching for goods sold through our online marketplace.”

— Item 1, Business

Note what “single-SKU” means here: every item is unique. There is no catalogue of repeated products. One handbag, one record, one price, one photograph — several million times a year.

The asset that cannot be bought

“Our online marketplace generates and aggregates hundreds of millions of unique data points, including data from over 50 million item sales since our inception. Each consigned item also has up to 50 unique attributes. Informed by this data, we have developed proprietary machine learning technology and business processes to optimize our operations, including supply sourcing, merchandising, authentication, pricing and marketing.”

— Item 1

Elsewhere the filing describes “proprietary item and consignor risk scoring algorithms” used to assist authentication in every category, and “AI and microphotography” used on high-end handbags.

The filing says it expects this expense “to increase in future periods to support our growth, including continuing to invest in automation and other technology improvements to support and drive efficiency in our operations.”

What the company protects, and how

“We rely on trademark, copyright, trade secrets, patents, patent applications, confidentiality agreements and other practices to protect our brands, proprietary information, technologies and processes… including the algorithms we use throughout our business.”

— Item 1, Intellectual Property

Who else is in the market

The company competes with “technology-enabled marketplaces that may offer the same or similar luxury goods and services,” and states it competes for consignors on “rich data and technology, and advanced authentication capabilities and expertise.”

Your question

Cash is tight and the platform spans authentication AI, pricing models, risk scoring, single-SKU inventory, payments and hosting. What do you build and what do you buy?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Invest hardest in the data itself — 50 million item sales, up to 50 attributes each — because it is what makes our authentication and pricing better than anyone else’s and it cannot be bought.
  • (B) Freeze platform investment until we are profitable — we are $23.9 million from breakeven and the technology already works.
  • (C) Build the authentication AI, the risk scoring and the single-SKU system ourselves; buy everything a consignor will never see — payments, hosting, email, back office.
  • (D) Buy a proven marketplace platform and configure it. We are a resale company, not a software company, and engineering is expensive.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the step on the chain your choice touches. SOURCE → VERIFY → PRICE → SELL → SHIP → RETURNS.
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Sort every system on this page into two piles before you answer: the ones a competitor could buy with a purchase order, and the ones they could not buy at any price. Your answer is what you do differently with the second pile.

Case: TheRealReal, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: what happens to authentication. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the building where the work happens. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The step you own

“We build trust through our authentication process. We continue to invest and innovate in authentication, both in our people and our technology. We believe we have the most rigorous authentication process in the resale luxury goods marketplace. We have highly trained gemologists, horologists, and brand experts who collectively inspect thousands of items each day. All items pass through a rigorous brand-specific authentication process before they are accepted for consignment.”

— Item 1, Business

The inspection looks for “appropriate brand markings, date codes, serial tags and hologram stickers.” Fine jewellery and watches ship with “an authentication certificate.” The company uses “AI and microphotography to assist in authenticating high-end handbags” and “proprietary item and consignor risk scoring algorithms” across every category.

“We have a zero-tolerance policy when it comes to counterfeit goods.”

What it costs

“We principally conduct our intake, authentication, merchandising and fulfillment operations in our leased authentication centers located in Arizona and New Jersey comprising an aggregate of approximately 1.4 million square feet of space.”

— Item 7

Operating expense, 2025 $ % of revenue
Operations and technology $275.9 M 40%
Selling, general and administrative $201.6 M 29%
Marketing $63.3 M 9%

Operations and technology is the largest expense line in the company — 40 cents of every revenue dollar. It covers authentication, merchandising, fulfillment, the retail stores and the platform.

The volume coming back

“We do not reduce GMV to reflect product returns or order cancellations, which totaled 24.2%, 24.4% and 26.4% of GMV in 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively.”

— Item 7

Roughly one dollar in four comes back — after the item has been authenticated, written up, photographed, priced, sold and shipped.

The cheaper way, and what the filing says about it

The company also runs a drop-ship consignment service, where goods never pass through its buildings:

“For inventory sold through our drop-ship consignment service that does not pass through our authentication centers, our authentication process includes diligence of our partners and procedures for establishing provenance, as well as quality checks and audits.”

— Item 1

And, from the risk factors:

“When our authentication method does not involve taking physical possession of goods prior to the sale, our ability to identify counterfeits may decrease, and order cancellations may increase.”

— Item 1A, Risk Factors

What is already working

“Improvements in our automation of authentication, pricing, copywriting and photo retouching have improved the efficiency of our operations.”

— Item 1

About 50% of products sell within 30 days of listing, and the sell-through ratio was over 80% in 2025.

Your question

Authentication runs through 1.4 million square feet and costs 40% of revenue. Every item gets a brand-specific physical check. What do you do with this step?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Attack the 24.2% return rate at intake — better condition grading, description and photography, so fewer authenticated items make the round trip twice.
  • (B) Move to sampling — full authentication above a price threshold, spot-checks below it. At 40% of revenue we cannot afford to inspect everything.
  • (C) Grow the drop-ship consignment service, where goods never pass through our buildings at all — it is the cheapest volume we can add.
  • (D) Automate inside the step — AI attribution, pricing, copywriting, photo retouching — so the cost per item falls while every item still gets its brand-specific physical check.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the step on the chain your choice touches. SOURCE → VERIFY → PRICE → SELL → SHIP → RETURNS.
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Somebody in this room is about to describe your 40% as bloat. They are not wrong that it is expensive. Ask yourself a different question: why does a stranger put a $5,000 handbag in a box and mail it to a company in Arizona? Whatever the answer is, that is the thing you are deciding about.

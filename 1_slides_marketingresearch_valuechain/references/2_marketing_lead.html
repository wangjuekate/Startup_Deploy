MENT 3235 · Module 1 — Marketing Research and the Value Chain Case: TheRealReal, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: where the marketing money goes. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is demand — and supply. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

Your budget

Marketing expense was $63.3 million in 2025 — 9% of revenue. The filing is specific about what that buys:

“Marketing expense comprises the cost of acquiring and retaining consignors and buyers, including the cost of television, digital and direct mail advertising.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

One budget. Two sides of a marketplace. That is your whole problem.

The buyers

2025 2024 2023
Active buyers (thousands) 1,056 972 922
Number of orders (thousands) 3,587 3,359 3,300
Average order value (AOV) $594 $545 $523

And the number somebody will point at: the company has “a global base of more than 40 million members” — but a member is only “any user who has registered an email address on our website or downloaded our mobile app.”

The supply side

“In 2025, over 80% of our gross merchandise value (‘GMV’) came from repeat consignors.”

— Item 1, Business

“We measure the ratio of demand versus supply in a given period, which we refer to as our online marketplace sell-through ratio… In 2025, our online marketplace sell-through ratio was over 80%.”

— Item 7

About 50% of products sell within 30 days of being listed. Over 80% of GMV came from repeat buyers as well.

The flywheel

“As of December 31, 2025, 16% of our buyers during the last twelve months also consigned items, and 50% of our consignors also made purchases.”

— Item 7

The filing calls this “the flywheel effect that strengthens the network dynamics of our online marketplace,” and notes it has built tools — the “Reconsign” module — to encourage it.

What the company is actually short of

“We grow our sales by increasing the supply of luxury goods offered through our consignment online marketplace… Our existing relationships with consignors allow us to unlock valuable supply across multiple categories.”

— Item 7

The market it is unlocking from: “a vast quantity of consignable luxury goods languishes in homes, and buyers can be hesitant to purchase pre-owned luxury goods.”

Your question

Marketing is $63.3 million — 9% of revenue — and it buys both buyers and consignors. Where should that money go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Into the category story — circular luxury and sustainability — to grow the whole resale market rather than fight for share of it.
  • (B) Into buyer acquisition — 1.06 million active buyers out of 40 million members is 2.6%, and that gap is the biggest number on the page.
  • (C) Into unlocking supply — recruiting consignors and turning existing buyers into consignors. Sell-through is over 80%, so goods are not what we are short of customers for; customers are what we are short of goods for.
  • (D) Into retention on both sides — over 80% of GMV is repeat, so losing a repeat consignor costs more than a new buyer earns.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the step on the chain your choice touches. SOURCE → VERIFY → PRICE → SELL → SHIP → RETURNS.
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

A marketplace has two sides and only one of them is usually the bottleneck. Before you spend a dollar attracting one more buyer, answer this out loud: if a thousand new buyers arrived tomorrow, what would we sell them?

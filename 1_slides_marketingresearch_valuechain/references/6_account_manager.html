Account Manager — Company Overview

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: TheRealReal, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You answer no functional question of your own. The other four leads each own one decision and one page. You own the whole company — and the order in which its problems get solved. Your page is the overview. Read it before they present.

The company, in the filing’s own terms

“The RealReal is the world’s largest online marketplace for authenticated, resale luxury goods. We are revolutionizing luxury resale by providing an end-to-end service that unlocks supply and creates a trusted, curated online marketplace for buyers globally.”

— Item 1, Business

Someone sends in a used luxury item. The company authenticates it, photographs it, writes it up, prices it, sells it, ships it — and keeps a percentage. The consignor keeps the rest.

The market it exists to fix:

“The existing luxury resale market is fragmented, difficult to access and laden with counterfeit goods. Primarily due to these challenges, a vast quantity of consignable luxury goods languishes in homes, and buyers can be hesitant to purchase pre-owned luxury goods.”

The industry it competes in:

“We compete with vendors of new and pre-owned luxury goods, including branded luxury goods stores, department stores, traditional brick-and-mortar consignment stores, pawn shops, auction houses, specialty retailers, discount chains, independent retail stores, the online offerings of traditional retail competitors, resale players focused on niche or single categories, as well as technology-enabled marketplaces… As the market evolves, new competitors may emerge, including traditional retail competitors who expand their offerings to include resale.”

— Item 1, Competition

It says it wins consignors on “rich data and technology, and advanced authentication capabilities and expertise.”

The thing that could end it: the filing states the company “cannot be certain that every counterfeit item will be identified,” that detection weakens where it does not take physical possession, and that a counterfeit sale “may damage our reputation as a trusted online marketplace.” Chanel’s counterfeiting and false-advertising claims remain unresolved.

The four problems on your table

Each lead will present one selection and one sentence of defense. These are the four:

Problem The lead who owns it
Money — which revenue line the next dollar feeds Financial Lead
Authentication — the step everything else depends on Operation Lead
Marketing — supply or demand Marketing Lead
Technology — what we build versus buy Tech Lead

Your task, in three parts

Part 1 — Rank them 1 to 4

Put the four problems in the order the company must resolve them to still be worth consigning to in five years. Each problem is used exactly once. Then give one sentence justifying rank #1 — and that sentence must contain a number from this page.

Part 2 — Weight them

Ranking says which comes first. Weighting says how much each one actually matters. Allocate 100 points across the four problems in blocks of 10. Your allocation must total exactly 100.

Explain why your highest priority receives its weight. An allocation such as 40/30/20/10 totals 100; the reasoning, not that particular pattern, is what matters.

Part 3 — Name the chokepoint

Out loud, to the team: which step on the chain — SOURCE → VERIFY → PRICE → SELL → SHIP → RETURNS — is the chokepoint here? And what would have to be true before you would let someone else perform it?

How to run the room

  • Rank by what the rest of the chain depends on, not by volume of voice. The most confident lead is not automatically right.
  • You may not rank #1 anything a lead could not defend with a number. If they gave you a feeling, send it back.
  • Watch for the growth-rate story. Somebody will tell you the fastest-growing line is the one to feed, and somebody else will tell you 1.06 million buyers out of 40 million members is the biggest opportunity on the page. Both are real numbers. Ask what each one keeps, and ask what the company is actually short of.
  • Watch for the efficiency story. Somebody will point at 40% of revenue and call it bloat. Ask them why a stranger mails a $5,000 handbag to Arizona.
  • You are allowed to disagree with your own team. The ranking is yours to sign.

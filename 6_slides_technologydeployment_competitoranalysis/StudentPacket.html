Three essential facts

  • The packet reports 86% of revenue from two related customers.
  • R&D was $243.3 million, about 48% of revenue.
  • Capacity delivery commitments must be matched to supply and infrastructure the company can actually obtain.

Three terms

  • Switching cost: what a customer gives up or must do to change suppliers.
  • Ecosystem: tools, partners, and skills surrounding a product.
  • Differentiation: a valuable reason to choose one offering over alternatives.

Worked example — discuss before opening the simulation

Illustrative startup: a tool saves five minutes per task but takes a team two days to learn. List the customer’s benefits and switching costs before claiming the new tool is better.

Readiness check: What customer outcome improves, and what must the customer give up to switch? Ask a teammate to explain the task back before starting.

Choose your role

Read the primer above and only your assigned role below. The detailed role pages and full company summary are optional references. Record the decision text, evidence, trade-off, and what would change your mind.

Financial Lead

Decision

Revenue grew 76%, margin fell to 39.0%, and the operating loss widened to $145.9 million. What do you do with the money?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Fix the margin on the growing line first. Cloud and services grew 94% at about 29.9% gross margin against hardware’s 42.9% — growth is arriving in the line that keeps least, and that is why total margin fell to 39.0%.
  • (B) We just earned $237.8 million of net income. Fund growth out of the profit and avoid dilution — the business is already paying for itself.
  • (C) Raise and spend on capacity. The prospectus says the cloud business needs significant data centre capacity and capital, and we cannot deliver the OpenAI tranches without it.
  • (D) Protect R&D at 48% of revenue whatever else happens — the speed advantage is the only reason any of these customers called us.

Need more evidence? Detailed role reference.

Marketing Lead

Decision

86% of revenue came from two related customers. Where does the go-to-market effort go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Into landing more flagship names like the OpenAI agreement. A reference customer that everyone respects is what makes a procurement committee brave.
  • (B) Into the channels buyers already use — AWS and the marketplaces and gateways — because being easy to try shrinks the switch, and many small customers is the only real cure for 86% of revenue sitting with two related parties.
  • (C) Into deepening the customers we already have. Our top ten customers grew their spend about 80% within 12 months — expanding them is far cheaper than winning new ones.
  • (D) Into the benchmark story — publish 15x inference and 10x training results everywhere, because speed is the only thing we have that nobody can copy this year.

Need more evidence? Detailed role reference.

Operation Lead

Decision

You have promised dated capacity on inputs nobody has promised you. Where does the operational effort go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Convert the supply chain from purchase orders to committed capacity — wafers, sole-source components, data-centre space — because we have promised dated capacity tranches on inputs nobody has promised us.
  • (B) Keep the flexible model. Purchase orders without volume commitments are what let a company our size scale up and down with demand.
  • (C) Put the effort into manufacturing yield and throughput at Sunnyvale, where we do final packaging, assembly and test ourselves — it is the part of the chain we actually own.
  • (D) Put the effort into the data-centre build itself. Leased space we do not control is the step between a signed contract and a delivered tranche.

Need more evidence? Detailed role reference.

Tech Lead

Decision

R&D is $243.3 million — 48% of revenue — and the hardware is already 15× faster. Where does the engineering go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Into the next generation of the WSE. The lead is the whole story, and the incumbent ships a new architecture every year.
  • (B) Into making the switch invisible — unchanged PyTorch, industry-standard APIs, the toolchains and gateways customers already use. We are already 15× faster; what stops adoption is everything they would have to throw away.
  • (C) Into custom benchmarks and reference designs that show the advantage as clearly as possible. If the numbers are unarguable, the market has to come to us.
  • (D) Into the software platform for the cloud business — orchestration, multi-tenancy and utilisation. It is the lower-margin line and engineering is how its cost curve changes.

Need more evidence? Detailed role reference.

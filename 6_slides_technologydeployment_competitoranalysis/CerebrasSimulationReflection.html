Stage 1 — Key takeaways

Define the adoption problem together

Technology Deployment & Competitor Analysis: Compare the buyer’s benefit with the full cost of switching.

  • Combine the four perspectives. The CFO holds the ceiling, the COO the capacity requirement, the CMO the channel costs, and the CTO the compatibility condition.
  • Separate performance from adoption. A faster chip does not remove a buyer’s code changes, retraining, procurement work, or delivery risk.
  • Make dependencies explicit. Channel listings require the drop-in layer; a delivery promise requires committed inputs. Ask who holds each missing fact.

  • D. Build cloud orchestration first to raise the cloud line’s margin.

Stage 2 — Key takeaways

Fund a route from speed to adoption

Technology Deployment & Competitor Analysis: Reduce switching costs and support delivery within one budget.

  • Coordinate the whole allocation. Committed supply (50) + the drop-in layer (30) + the channel program (20) use the 100-unit ceiling.
  • Connect engineering to the customer. Unchanged code and familiar interfaces enable channel access; the channel program then reaches many smaller buyers.
  • Respect the financial constraint. Non-operating income adds no planning units. A 70-unit next-generation chip would leave too little for the 50-unit capacity commitment.

Use each question once. Explain your first priority using information an executive shared through team chat.

Stage 3 — Key takeaways

Prioritize the constraints on adoption

Technology Deployment & Competitor Analysis: Use company dependencies to explain the ranking.

  • Put Delivery and Concentration first. Securing the promised tranche and broadening the customer base address the two immediate constraints in this scenario.
  • Keep Money ahead of Engineering. The shared ceiling binds the plan. Engineering ranks fourth in the template because its job is to enable the channel, not pursue speed alone.
  • Explain what would change your view. Ask whether buyers can integrate the service, whether new customers reduce concentration, and whether committed capacity arrives on time.

Marketing Lead

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Cerebras Systems Inc., IPO prospectus (Form 424B4, May 13, 2026)

You own one decision: where the go-to-market effort goes. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is who buys, and how many of them there are. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The customer list is very short

“A substantial portion of our revenue is driven by a limited number of customers. Group 42 Holding Ltd (together with its affiliates, ‘G42’) accounted for 24.0% and 85.0% of our total revenue for the years ended December 31, 2025 and 2024, respectively, and in the year ended December 31, 2025, Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (‘MBZUAI’) accounted for 62.0% of our total revenue.”

— Risk Factors

That is 86% of 2025 revenue in two names. And:

“G42 and MBZUAI are considered related parties with respect to each other.”

“As of December 31, 2025, one customer (MBZUAI) accounted for 77.9% of our accounts receivable balance. As of December 31, 2024, one customer (G42) accounted for 91.0%.”

So it is not two independent customers. It is one relationship wearing two names — and most of the money the company is still owed sits inside it.

The names that are supposed to change that

“OpenAI, the world’s leading foundation model lab, selected us to be its fast inference solution. With Cerebras, OpenAI’s Codex-Spark users turn ideas into working software in seconds.”

The December 2025 agreement with OpenAI “represents a substantial portion of our projected revenues over the next several years” — projected, not yet earned.

“Amazon Web Services (‘AWS’), the world’s leading hyperscale cloud, has signed a binding term sheet with us to become the first hyperscaler to deploy Cerebras in its own data centers, providing massive distribution to a broad base of enterprise customers.”

The routes that already exist

“Our high-speed inference services are available through partners, including AWS Marketplace, Microsoft Marketplace, IBM watsonx Model Gateway, Vercel AI Gateway, OpenRouter, and Hugging Face, enabling seamless adoption within existing workflows.”

Read that last phrase again. Those channels are not advertising. They are the places where the customer is already buying, with a purchase order that already exists and a vendor that has already been approved.

The number people will quote at you

“Our top ten customers by year-to-date revenue through December 31, 2025 increased their aggregate spend with us by approximately 80% within 12 months of their initial purchase, often including contracts for co-development.”

Why customers stay once they arrive

“Once customers adopt fast inference, user expectations for interactivity rise, and engineering teams shift from latency optimizations to other work, making it difficult to return to slower inference.”

The market, and who else is selling into it

“The combined market… is estimated to be $251 billion in 2025 and is expected to grow to $672 billion by 2029—a 28% CAGR… AI inference will grow more than twice as fast as AI training infrastructure through 2029.”

“Our hardware primarily competes against solutions from NVIDIA…, Advanced Micro Devices…, Intel… We also compete against full-service cloud service providers such as Amazon.com, Inc. (AWS), Microsoft Corporation (Azure), Alphabet Inc. (Google Cloud Platform), and Oracle Corporation, as well as AI-optimized specialized clouds such as CoreWeave, Inc.”

“Some of our competitors have greater marketing, financial, distribution, and manufacturing resources than we do.”

Your question

86% of revenue came from two related customers. Where does the go-to-market effort go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Into landing more flagship names like the OpenAI agreement. A reference customer that everyone respects is what makes a procurement committee brave.
  • (B) Into the channels buyers already use — AWS and the marketplaces and gateways — because being easy to try shrinks the switch, and many small customers is the only real cure for 86% of revenue sitting with two related parties.
  • (C) Into deepening the customers we already have. Our top ten customers grew their spend about 80% within 12 months — expanding them is far cheaper than winning new ones.
  • (D) Into the benchmark story — publish 15x inference and 10x training results everywhere, because speed is the only thing we have that nobody can copy this year.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Say which side of the switching equation your choice moves — our advantage, or the customer’s cost of leaving.
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

One test before you commit: if the single relationship behind 86% of last year’s revenue stopped tomorrow, what would you point at? Whatever you point at is what your budget should be building.

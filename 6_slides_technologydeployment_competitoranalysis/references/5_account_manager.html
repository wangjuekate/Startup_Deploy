Account Manager — Company Overview

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Cerebras Systems Inc., IPO prospectus (Form 424B4, May 13, 2026)

You answer no functional question of your own. The other four leads each own one decision and one page. You own the whole company — and the order in which its problems get solved. Your page is the overview. Read it before they present.

The company, in the filing’s own terms

“We are building the fastest AI infrastructure in the world. In AI, speed is critical to win… Our solutions are built for speed. Cerebras Inference delivers answers up to 15 times faster than leading GPU-based solutions… many customers have achieved more than 10 times faster training time-to-solution.”

“These performance breakthroughs are the result of our core innovation: the world’s first and only commercialized wafer-scale processor… 58 times larger than NVIDIA’s B200 chip and… 2,625 times more memory bandwidth… we solved the 75-year-old compute industry problem of wafer-scale integration.”

It sells three ways: on-premises supercomputers, Cerebras Cloud, and partner channels (AWS Marketplace, Microsoft Marketplace, IBM watsonx, Vercel, OpenRouter, Hugging Face). Chips are fabricated by TSMC under a fabless model; final packaging and test happen in Sunnyvale. 708 employees.

Why a prospectus and not a 10-K: Cerebras IPO’d on May 14, 2026 and has not yet filed an annual report. This document carries the audited FY2025 and FY2024 financial statements.

Revenue path: $24.6 M (2022) → $78.7 M (2023) → $290.3 M (2024) → $510.0 M (2025).

Three things to hold at once: 1. The reported net income of $237.8 M came from $390.7 M of other income. The operating result was a $145.9 M loss, wider than last year. Non-GAAP: a $75.7 M net loss. 2. Gross margin fell from 42.3% to 39.0% — hardware keeps ~42.9%, cloud and services ~29.9%, and cloud is the faster-growing line. 3. R&D is 48% of revenue.

The customers: MBZUAI 62.0% and G42 24.0% of 2025 revenue — “related parties with respect to each other” — and MBZUAI was 77.9% of accounts receivable. (G42 was 85.0% of revenue in 2024 and 91.0% of receivables.)

The commitments: the December 2025 OpenAI MRA is “a substantial portion of our projected revenues over the next several years”, requires dated capacity tranches, gives OpenAI the right “to terminate a portion or all of the agreement” on failure to deliver, and carries a $1.0 billion Working Capital Loan at 6% repayable immediately on termination. AWS has a binding term sheet to be “the first hyperscaler to deploy Cerebras in its own data centers.”

The supply: “third-party suppliers, including certain sole sources… procured on a purchase order basis without capacity or volume commitments.” Data centres are leased and not controlled (California, Oklahoma, Canada).

The market: “$251 billion in 2025… $672 billion by 2029—a 28% CAGR… inference will grow more than twice as fast as training.”

The competition: NVIDIA, AMD, Intel, hyperscaler accelerators, AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, Oracle, CoreWeave — and “some of our competitors have greater marketing, financial, distribution, and manufacturing resources than we do.”

The legal exposure: U.S. export controls (EAR/BIS, OFAC) — “we cannot guarantee that we will be successful in obtaining all required licenses” — and disclosed material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting.

Part 3 — Name the dimension

Out loud, to the team: what is the buyer actually bleeding on — and is our advantage on that dimension bigger than everything the buyer would have to throw away (the complements, the skills, the habits, the career risk)?

   ADVANTAGE (on the dimension they buy)   vs   MOAT (what they abandon)

How to run the room

  • Rank by what would stop this company selling, not by volume of voice.
  • You may not rank #1 anything a lead could not defend with a number.
  • Watch for “now that we’re profitable.” Ask where the $237.8 million came from. The operating line was a $145.9 million loss.
  • Watch for the expansion story. Somebody will say the top ten customers grew 80% in twelve months, so sell them more. Ask how the company got to 86% of revenue in two related parties.
  • Watch for the benchmark story. Somebody will want to widen a 15× lead. Ask them to name the customer who said no because 15× was not enough.
  • Watch for “we’re only 708 people.” That is the argument that produces material weaknesses — which this company has already disclosed.
  • You are allowed to disagree with your own team. The ranking is yours to sign.

Case: Cerebras Systems Inc., IPO prospectus (Form 424B4, May 13, 2026)

You own one decision: what to do with the money. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the income statement. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The growth is real

Revenue: $24.6 million (2022) → $78.7 million (2023) → $290.3 million (2024) → $510.0 million (2025). The last year was +76%.

Work down that table slowly. Three things happen on the way.

One — gross margin fell. $199.1 M on $510.0 M is 39.0%; last year it was 42.3%. Revenue grew 76% and each dollar of it kept less.

Two — the loss from operations got bigger, not smaller: $(145.9) million against $(101.4) million.

Three — the net income of $237.8 million came from the line above it. “Other income (expense), net” of $390.7 million. The company reports its own alternative figure:

“We incurred non-GAAP net loss of $75.7 million in 2025 and $21.8 million in 2024, after excluding the impact of stock-based compensation expense and change in fair value (extinguishment) of forward contract liability.”

Why the margin fell — look at the mix

2025 Revenue Cost Keeps
Hardware $358.4 M $204.7 M ~42.9%
Cloud and other services $151.6 M (up 94%) $106.2 M ~29.9%

The faster-growing line is the lower-margin one. That is the whole explanation for a company growing 76% while its margin falls.

What the money is being asked to pay for

  • Research and development: $243.3 million — about 48% of revenue.
  • The cloud business “require[s] significant data center capacity and capital investments for which we expect to require significant additional capital.”
  • Under the OpenAI agreement the company received a $1.0 billion Working Capital Loan at 6%, repayable in installments after final delivery of the initial capacity — and immediately if the agreement terminates.
  • 708 employees as of December 31, 2025.

Your question

Revenue grew 76%, margin fell to 39.0%, and the operating loss widened to $145.9 million. What do you do with the money?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Fix the margin on the growing line first. Cloud and services grew 94% at about 29.9% gross margin against hardware’s 42.9% — growth is arriving in the line that keeps least, and that is why total margin fell to 39.0%.
  • (B) We just earned $237.8 million of net income. Fund growth out of the profit and avoid dilution — the business is already paying for itself.
  • (C) Raise and spend on capacity. The prospectus says the cloud business needs significant data centre capacity and capital, and we cannot deliver the OpenAI tranches without it.
  • (D) Protect R&D at 48% of revenue whatever else happens — the speed advantage is the only reason any of these customers called us.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Say which side of the switching equation your choice moves — our advantage, or the customer’s cost of leaving.
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Somebody in this room is going to open with “now that we’re profitable.” Before you let that sentence past, find the line on the table above where the profit came from, and say it out loud.

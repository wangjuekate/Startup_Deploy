Cerebras: Debrief

Use after discussion. These takeaways explain the reasoning; compare them with your original decision.

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Funding must support a route to adoption. Explain how the proposed spending strengthens the offering, makes switching easier or enables delivery.
  • Compare financial measures carefully. Distinguish revenue, margin, operating results and available cash before assessing the resources behind the plan.
  • Test the investment hypothesis. Identify what customer or cost evidence would justify further spending and what would make you change course.

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The buyer’s problem defines the comparison. Specify the customer segment, the problem it values solving and the evidence that the offering addresses it.
  • Map the cost of leaving. Consider procurement, skills, workflows, complements and perceived risk when judging the route to market.
  • Assess the customer mix. Explain how the proposed channel changes both adoption and dependence on particular accounts.

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Delivery is part of the switching decision. Explain what a buyer must believe about capacity, timing and support before relying on a new supplier.
  • Map promises to dependencies. Identify what has been promised to customers, what inputs the plan depends on and who controls those inputs.
  • Compare flexibility and assurance. State what the proposal commits, what it leaves adjustable and how you would respond if demand or supply changes.

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Benchmark the dimension that matters. Explain which workload and user need the performance measure represents, and what the benchmark leaves out.
  • Compare both sides of the threshold. Assess the expected benefit of the proposed engineering work against the customer effort it creates or removes.
  • Make the adoption hypothesis testable. Identify a trial that would reveal whether the remaining obstacle is performance, integration, cost or something else.

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • A technical advantage matters when customers can realize it after switching costs.
  • Different rankings can be defensible. Connect the priorities to evidence and the weekly concept.
  • Example allocation: 40/30/20/10 totals 100. Explain your own allocation; priority points are not course-grade points.

Key takeaway

  • Return to the keyboard example. Compare a benefit on the dimension a buyer values with the full cost of switching, then explain which part of that comparison your team’s plan changes.
  • What would make a particular customer choose to switch?

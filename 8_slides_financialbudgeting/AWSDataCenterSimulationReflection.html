Marvell's Commitment Ladder

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, November 12

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • As a group, name the bucket your decision sits in — COMMITTED, STICKY or FLEXIBLE — and how long it takes to stop.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and defense.
💰

Financial Lead

$2.5 billion came in from the divestiture. Cash is ~$2.6 B; borrowings $4.5 B with $499.8 M due within twelve months; a $1.5 B revolver is undrawn; a dividend and buybacks are running. $1,871.8 M is owed to foundries in fiscal 2027, and ~$1.6 B of cash has just gone on Celestial and XConn. Where does the money go?

  • A Hold enough against the $1,871.8 M of fiscal 2027 foundry commitments.
  • B Retire debt — $4.5 B outstanding, $202.6 M of interest expense.
  • C Fund the acquisitions and Celestial's contingent payments through fiscal 2029.
  • D Return it to shareholders — 42% growth, 32.6% net margin, strongest balance sheet ever.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The 32.6% net margin is not an operating margin: operating income was 16.1% of revenue, and most of the difference is the $1.8 billion divestiture gain sitting in "interest income and other."
  • Match the funding to the least reversible obligation — $1,871.8 M is due in the year that just started and is paid regardless of demand.
  • Returning the proceeds converts the company's remaining flexibility into a payout, in the same year it owes $1.87 B it cannot cancel for free.
📣

Marketing Lead

Distributor A is 37% of revenue (from 24% two years ago); Direct Customer A is 14%; four customers = 73% of receivables; 77% of revenue goes to Asia. Export restrictions are expected to keep biting, with customers stockpiling or building "indigenous solutions." Data center grew 46%. Where does commercial effort go?

  • A Widening the customer base.
  • B Securing longer, firmer commitments from the largest customers.
  • C Into the data center market as hard as possible.
  • D Supporting the customers building inventory ahead of export restrictions.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Concentration is worsening, not stable: Distributor A went 24% → 34% → 37%. A wider base is what makes a multi-year ladder defensible.
  • Committed costs against cancellable revenue is the mismatch at the centre of the case — firmer customer commitments attack it from the demand side, but customers buy on purchase orders precisely because they want the option.
  • The inventory build is borrowed demand — customers stockpiling ahead of export restrictions are pulling next year's orders into this year, and the foundry commitments behind them are real either way.
⚙️

Operation Lead

$2,665.8 M of foundry commitments, $1,871.8 M of it in fiscal 2027 alone. Capacity reservations run 4–10 years with at least $458.2 M through fiscal 2033. Cancelling costs money and queue priority. Most qualified foundries sit in one region of Taiwan. What do you do with the ladder?

  • A Re-shape it — stagger commitments across years instead of stacking them.
  • B Diversify where it is made.
  • C Negotiate the exit terms rather than the volumes.
  • D Commit more capacity now while it is available.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Seventy percent of all commitments land in one year: $1,871.8 M of $2,665.8 M — a single demand forecast with $1.87 B riding on it. Staggering converts one enormous bet into a series of revisable ones.
  • You can cancel — you just pay: cancellation costs incurred expenses, possible fees, forfeited advances, and loss of queue priority, which may be the most expensive item on the list.
  • The two sides of the ladder have different speeds: customers cancel on relatively short notice; foundries are booked for 4–10 years. Revenue falls fast; costs fall slowly.
💻

Tech Lead

R&D is $2,075.2 M — 25.3% of revenue, down from 33.9% because revenue grew faster than spending ($409.0 M of it is stock compensation). Celestial (~$1.3 B + 24.5M shares, contingent through fiscal 2029) and XConn ($280 M + shares) have just arrived. How do you budget engineering?

  • A Stage it against committed design wins.
  • B Concentrate on integrating what we just bought.
  • C Hold the dollar amount flat and let the ratio keep falling.
  • D Spend up to keep the ratio where the industry expects it.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Engineering is STICKY money — you cannot stop a programme mid-flight without losing the socket and the team. Staging each tranche against a committed design win keeps sticky spending anchored to real demand.
  • The ratio did not fall because spending was cut — R&D rose $124.8 M; it fell because revenue grew 42%. Budgeting to a percentage of revenue is budgeting to a forecast.
  • ~$1.6 B plus shares plus contingencies through fiscal 2029 is already spent — unintegrated technology is the most expensive shelf-ware there is.
🔒

Security Lead

Export restrictions on certain Chinese customers are expected "to continue to impact our revenue"; most qualified foundries are in one region of Taiwan, where disruption "will adversely impact our business"; the cyber programme follows NIST CSF with board-level reporting. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Fund export-control compliance and design-IP protection as committed lines, ahead of discretionary growth programmes.
  • B Fund geographic resilience — qualify alternative foundry and assembly locations.
  • C Fund the cyber programme's maturity against the NIST framework.
  • D Fund security from what is left after the growth programmes.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Sequence the budget: these are the exposures that decide whether the committed spending is worth anything — export restrictions are expected to continue to impact revenue, and for a fabless company the designs are the asset.
  • "No material incident and a strong year" is the argument that puts the only non-discretionary risk lines last in the queue.
  • Geographic resilience is expensive and slow — which is exactly why it must be budgeted rather than intended.
🧭

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question.

After all five leads present their selection + one-sentence defense, rank the five decisions 1–5 by which the company must resolve first, allocate 100 points across them in blocks of 10, and total the committed bucket: sort every major spending line into COMMITTED / STICKY / FLEXIBLE, add up the committed bucket for fiscal 2027, and say how much of next year is already decided.

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Rank by reversibility — commitments and money belong at the top, and the rank-#1 sentence must contain a figure.
  • Signed for years, sold by purchase order: obligations are fixed for years; revenue is cancellable in weeks. That asymmetry is the whole budgeting problem.
  • Teams that argue from the 32.6% net margin will over-commit — the $1.8 B gain happens once; the $2,075.2 M of R&D happens every year.

Key takeaway

Budget by how fast you can undo a decision, not by percent of revenue. Sort every line into committed, sticky and flexible — and total the committed bucket against worst-case revenue, not the forecast.

A great year is exactly when flexible money becomes committed money.

Case: Marvell Technology, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You own one decision: how to budget engineering. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the stickiest money in the company. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

Your budget

FY2026 FY2025 Change
Research and development $2,075.2 M $1,950.4 M +6.4%
As a percentage of net revenue 25.3% 33.9% —
Net revenue $8,194.6 M $5,767.3 M +42.1%

Read those two rows together before anyone in the room tells you R&D was cut.

“Research and development expense increased by $124.8 million in fiscal 2026 compared to fiscal 2025. The increase was primarily due to higher overall spending to support our R&D initiatives, including advanced IP development and customer design win activity.”

The dollars went up. The ratio went down — because revenue grew 42%. Those are two very different facts, and only one of them is a decision.

Of that budget, $409.0 million was stock-based compensation (out of $590.8 million company-wide).

What just landed on your desk

Subsequent to the fiscal 2026 year end, on February 2, 2026, the company completed the acquisition of Celestial AI, Inc. — “a provider of a Photonic Fabric™ technology platform purpose-built for next-generation scale-up interconnect… expected to accelerate our connectivity strategy for next-generation AI and cloud data centers.”

Paid: approximately $1.3 billion in cash (about $1.0 billion net of cash acquired) plus approximately 24.5 million shares. “Contingent on the achievement of specified revenue milestones, we may be required to pay additional cash and issue additional shares of our common stock through fiscal 2029.”

On February 10, 2026, the company completed the acquisition of XConn Technologies — “a provider of advanced PCIe and CXL switching silicon, which expands our switching portfolio and augments our Ultra Accelerator Link (‘UALink™’) scale-up switch team.” Paid approximately $280.0 million in cash plus approximately 2.1 million shares.

Two acquisitions, ten days apart, roughly $1.6 billion of cash — plus contingent obligations running to fiscal 2029.

The company you are engineering for

Net revenue $8,194.6 M (+42.1%); data center +46% on AI demand; gross margin 51.0%, up from 41.3%. Operating income 16.1% of revenue. The company is fabless and operates in one reportable segment.

The 32.6% net margin includes a $1.8 billion pre-tax gain from selling the automotive ethernet business for $2.5 billion in cash — so the operating result is the 16.1%, not the 32.6%.

And the commitments your roadmap sits on top of

$2,665.8 million of unconditional purchase commitments to foundries and test and assembly partners — $1,871.8 million in fiscal 2027 alone — plus $635.3 million of technology services and licence fees and $152.0 million of capital expenditure commitments. Capacity reservations run 4 to 10 years.

Meanwhile the demand: “a significant portion of our sales are made on a purchase order basis, which allows our customers to cancel, change or delay product purchase commitments with relatively short notice.”

Why your money is the stickiest

The filing describes R&D spending driven by “advanced IP development and customer design win activity.” A design win is a customer choosing your silicon for a product they will ship in two or three years. Stop the programme halfway and you do not save the money you already spent — you lose the socket, the customer, and the team that knew how to build it.

Your question

R&D is $2.08 billion and it is the hardest money in the company to stop. How do you budget it?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Stage it against committed design wins. R&D is the stickiest money we have — stopping a programme mid-flight loses the socket and the team — so each stage should be tied to a customer commitment that already exists.
  • (B) Hold the R&D dollar amount flat and let it fall as a share of revenue. It has already gone from 33.9% to 25.3% and the business grew anyway.
  • (C) Spend up to keep the ratio where the industry expects it. If revenue is $8.2 billion, R&D should scale with it, and cutting the ratio signals retreat.
  • (D) Concentrate on integrating what we just bought. Celestial and XConn cost about $1.6 billion plus contingent payments through fiscal 2029, and unintegrated acquisitions are the most expensive shelf-ware there is.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the bucket — is this COMMITTED (months to never), STICKY (a quarter or two, with damage), or FLEXIBLE (stoppable immediately)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Before you argue for a number, answer this: how long would it take to stop a design-win programme halfway, and what exactly would we get back? If the answer is “eighteen months and nothing,” you are not budgeting a cost — you are budgeting a commitment, and it should be staged like one.

Case: Marvell Technology, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You own one decision: where the money goes. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the cash. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

Somebody will read the bottom row as a 32.6% net margin. Read the row above it first.

“On August 14, 2025, we completed the sale of our automotive ethernet business to Infineon Technologies AG for $2.5 billion in cash. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, we recorded a pre-tax gain on sale of $1.8 billion, which is included in interest income and other, net.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

The operating business earned 16.1%. The rest came from selling a division.

What you have

“Our principal source of liquidity as of January 31, 2026 consisted of approximately $2.6 billion of cash and cash equivalents, of which approximately $642.0 million was held by subsidiaries outside of the United States.”

“As of January 31, 2026, we had total borrowings outstanding of $4.5 billion… of which $499.8 million are due within twelve months.”

The company repaid $590.6 million of a term loan during the year, issued $500 million of 2030 notes and $500 million of 2035 notes in June 2025, and has a $1.5 billion revolving credit facility, undrawn, available through June 30, 2030. Operating cash flow was $1.8 billion. It pays a quarterly dividend and repurchases stock, both “subject to… our results of operations, cash balances and future cash requirements.”

And it is not free to change your mind:

“Cancellation of outstanding purchase orders is allowed but requires payment of all costs and expenses incurred through the date of cancellation, and in some cases, may result in incremental fees, loss of amounts paid in advance, or loss of priority to reserved capacity.”

What you just spent

  • Celestial AI (February 2, 2026): approximately $1.3 billion in cash (about $1.0 billion net of cash acquired) plus ~24.5 million shares. “Contingent on the achievement of specified revenue milestones, we may be required to pay additional cash and issue additional shares… through fiscal 2029.”
  • XConn (February 10, 2026): approximately $280.0 million in cash plus ~2.1 million shares.

And what the revenue rests on

Distributor A was 37% of net revenue (34% and 24% in the two prior years); Direct Customer A 14%; four customers were 73% of gross accounts receivable. And:

“A significant portion of our sales are made on a purchase order basis, which allows our customers to cancel, change or delay product purchase commitments with relatively short notice to us.”

Your question

Where does the money go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Fund the acquisitions and the contingent payments. Celestial and XConn cost about $1.6 billion in cash, and Celestial may require more cash and shares through fiscal 2029.
  • (B) Retire debt. $4.5 billion is outstanding with $499.8 million due within twelve months, and interest expense was $202.6 million — permanently reducing the fixed charge lowers the floor every future year has to clear.
  • (C) Hold enough cash against the $1,871.8 million of foundry commitments due in fiscal 2027. It is the least reversible obligation on the books and it gets paid whether or not the demand behind it shows up.
  • (D) Return it to shareholders through buybacks and dividends. We grew 42%, earned a 32.6% net margin, and the balance sheet has never looked stronger.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the bucket — is this COMMITTED (months to never), STICKY (a quarter or two, with damage), or FLEXIBLE (stoppable immediately)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

One question before you commit anything: if data center revenue is flat next year instead of up 46%, what still has to be paid? Total that number. It is the only part of the budget that is not a suggestion.

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Marvell Technology, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You own one decision: where the commercial effort goes. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is who actually buys. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The year was extraordinary

Net revenue $8,194.6 million, up 42.1%. “This was primarily due to a 46% increase in sales from the data center end market which benefited from strong AI-related demand.” Communications and other grew 31%.

Through how many hands

Net revenue attributable to significant customers whose revenues represented 10% or more of total net revenue:

Share of total net revenue FY2026 FY2025 FY2024
Distributor A 37% 34% 24%
Direct Customer A 14% 13% less than 10%

“The Company’s accounts receivable was concentrated with four customers at January 31, 2026, who represented a total of 73% of gross accounts receivable.”

“Sales shipped to customers with operations in Asia represented approximately 77% and 75% of our net revenue in fiscal 2026 and 2025.”

Watch the direction on Distributor A: 24% → 34% → 37%. The concentration is not stable. It is getting worse each year.

And on what terms

“A significant portion of our sales are made on a purchase order basis, which allows our customers to cancel, change or delay product purchase commitments with relatively short notice to us.”

— Item 1A, Risk Factors

The filing also warns that customers “may decide not to purchase our products at all, purchase fewer products than they did in the past, or alter their purchasing patterns.”

Now put that next to what the company has promised to pay

$2,665.8 million of unconditional purchase commitments to foundries and test and assembly partners — $1,871.8 million of it in fiscal 2027 alone. Capacity reservation agreements run 4 to 10 years. Cancelling “requires payment of all costs and expenses incurred through the date of cancellation” plus possible fees, forfeited advances and loss of queue priority.

Your customers can cancel in weeks. Your foundries cannot be cancelled for years.

The policy weather

“We expect that the U.S. government’s export restrictions on certain Chinese customers to continue to impact our revenue. Moreover, concerns that U.S. companies may not be reliable suppliers as a result of these and other actions has caused, and may in the future cause, some of our customers in China to amass large inventories of our products well in advance of need or cause some of our customers to replace our products in favor of products from other suppliers. Customers in China may also choose to develop indigenous solutions, as replacements for products that are subject to U.S. export controls.”

— Item 7

Read the middle clause carefully. Some of this year’s orders are not this year’s demand. They are next year’s demand, bought early, by customers who are afraid you will not be allowed to sell to them.

One more thing about the great year

The 32.6% net margin includes a $1.8 billion pre-tax gain on selling the automotive ethernet business to Infineon for $2.5 billion in cash. Operating income was 16.1% of revenue.

Your question

One distributor is 37% of revenue, and everything behind it is committed for years. Where does the commercial effort go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Into securing longer, firmer commitments from the largest customers, so the demand side of the ladder starts to look like the supply side.
  • (B) Into widening the customer base. Our costs are committed for years and our revenue arrives on purchase orders that can be cancelled with short notice — and one distributor has gone from 24% to 37% of revenue in two years.
  • (C) Into supporting the customers building inventory ahead of export restrictions. They are buying now, they are buying a lot, and revenue booked is revenue booked.
  • (D) Into the data center market as hard as possible. It grew 46% and that is where the AI demand behind every one of these commitments actually is.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the bucket — is this COMMITTED (months to never), STICKY (a quarter or two, with damage), or FLEXIBLE (stoppable immediately)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Ask yourself one question and answer it honestly: how long would it take Distributor A to stop ordering, and how long would it take us to stop paying the foundries? The gap between those two numbers is your job.

NYT v. Microsoft & OpenAI — Suing the Chart

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, October 1

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • Describe contested conduct as "the complaint alleges". Distinguish allegations, evidence and requested remedies from established findings.
  • As a group, defend your decision and name the corner of the trilemma it buys — and the corner it pays with.
  • Cite a specific detail from the complaint, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and reasoning.

Financial Lead

Capital never arrives alone. Where does the next dollar come from — and what does it take?

  • A Keep the charitable parent nominally in control on the org chart and let the for-profit subsidiaries take whatever terms investors require.
  • B Raise against a defined scope, and treat the governance rights the investor wants as part of the price — because the complaint reaches Microsoft through "legal and financial influence", not through its stake.
  • C Take the strategic money and the compute together — it is the cheapest capital available and the alternative is not building the thing at all.
  • D Diversify the capital base so no single provider holds both the money and the infrastructure we cannot operate without.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Capital comes with terms. Identify the decision rights, dependencies and financial obligations attached to your proposed funding source.
  • Use all three corners. Explain how the proposal affects control, access to capital and exposure, including the questions the packet leaves unresolved.
  • Test the arrangement under disagreement. Identify who could refuse a proposed action and what would happen if the partners wanted different things.

Marketing Lead

Your company's structure is about to change. When a stated purpose is a legal fact, what do you say?

  • A Keep the founding mission language exactly as it is. It is what the brand was built on, and changing it would concede that something changed.
  • B Publish the governance arrangements themselves — who controls what, and what the mission constrains — so the promise and the chart cannot drift apart.
  • C Say less about purpose altogether and let the products speak, since any mission language can be read back as a commitment.
  • D Say only what the structure can actually deliver, and restate it the day the structure changes — because a stated purpose is a charter document, and this complaint quotes the 2015 promise back at a 2023 defendant.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Promises and governance must be examined together. Identify what the proposed statement commits the organization to and who has authority to deliver it.
  • Distinguish the documents. Compare public messaging, governance arrangements and the purposes described in the packet without treating them as interchangeable.
  • Plan for change. Explain how the team would review its statements when its funding, structure or activities change.

Operation Lead

A licence converts an unbounded exposure into a priced line item. How do you obtain your inputs?

  • A Build the provenance system first — know exactly what went in and under what terms — so we can licence, exclude or replace any input on demand.
  • B Take the public web now and negotiate later from a position of strength — the product exists either way, and a settlement after launch is cheaper than a licence before it.
  • C Licence the inputs before the build, on defined-purpose terms — the market rate was published and the counterparty asked to negotiate in April 2023; a priced contract term is the only version of this exposure that has a ceiling.
  • D Rely on the doctrinal argument that training is transformative, and reserve against the risk that a court disagrees.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Inputs carry terms as well as costs. Identify what your proposed approach requires the company to know about an input and its permitted uses.
  • Compare the tradeoffs. Explain how each relevant cost, restriction and uncertainty affects control, capital and exposure under your proposal.
  • Separate the claim from the outcome. Distinguish the complaint's allegations and requested relief from what has been established in the supplied material.

Tech Lead

Liability attaches to control, not to the org chart. How much control do you take over a partner's system?

  • A Stay a neutral utility: general-purpose infrastructure, no bespoke build, no involvement in what the customer runs on it.
  • B Push the whole obligation into the contract — warranties on the training data, audit rights, and indemnities that follow whoever chose the inputs.
  • C Take as much operational control as the partnership will give us and rely on the separate corporate entities to keep the liability where it belongs.
  • D Decide deliberately: either hold the control and exercise it — with real input review and the ability to say no — or contract it away and stay a utility. The pleaded theory is "right and ability to prevent", so unexercised control is the worst of both.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Control has a practical meaning. Map who designs, operates, monitors and can stop the system, then compare those roles with the agreements.
  • Assess the chosen boundary. Explain how your proposal changes capability, dependence and responsibility between the partners.
  • Specify evidence and escalation. Identify what information a decision-maker needs and who acts when the partnership encounters a disputed input or use.
🔒

Security Lead

Entity boundaries, indemnities and insurance all move money. This remedy is not money. How do you contain it?

  • A Hold the models in a separate subsidiary so a judgment against it cannot reach the parent or the rest of the group.
  • B Contract for it — indemnities from whoever supplied the data, warranties, and a commitment to defend customers, so the exposure at least has an owner.
  • C Reserve and insure against it, and treat the exposure as a quantified liability on the balance sheet like any other.
  • D Engineer the asset so the exposure is separable — provenance records, the ability to identify and remove a source, retrainable checkpoints — because no entity boundary and no policy stands between a court and a § 503(b) order.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Different consequences require different plans. Distinguish financial loss, operational interruption and restrictions affecting the product when assessing the requested remedy.
  • Test the scope of the proposed response. Explain which consequences your plan addresses and which remain unresolved.
  • Connect the response to governance. Identify the records, authority and coordination needed to act, while distinguishing requested relief from a court's decision.

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Read the structure as a set of relationships. Explain who supplies capital, who can decide and where the team sees exposure, using the complaint as a source of allegations.
  • Priorities need a shared basis. Ranking sets order; weighting expresses relative emphasis. Show how the five choices affect one another.
  • Name the exchange. Identify which corner the proposed arrangement gains, what it gives up and the assumptions that would make the trade acceptable.

Key takeaway

Use the Structure Trilemma to examine the arrangements in the case: who funds, who decides and who faces which consequences. Support the analysis with the supplied complaint while keeping allegations and legal outcomes distinct.

NYT v. Microsoft & OpenAI — Suing the Chart

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, October 1

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • Every statement of fact must begin "the complaint alleges" — hold each other to it out loud.
  • As a group, defend your decision and name the corner of the trilemma it buys — and the corner it pays with.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and defense.
💰

Financial Lead

The complaint pleads a nonprofit that raised "$1 billion in seed money," converted in 2019 "to rais[e] capital from investors seeking a return," and a structure that "grant[s] OpenAI's investors… authority and influence over OpenAI's operations." Microsoft has invested "at least $13 billion." Where does the next dollar of capital come from?

  • A Raise against a defined scope, treating the governance rights the investor wants as part of the price.
  • B Diversify so no single provider holds both the money and the infrastructure we depend on.
  • C Take the strategic money and the compute together — cheapest capital available.
  • D Keep the charitable parent nominally in control on the chart and let the subsidiaries take whatever terms investors require.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The $13 billion is pleaded as evidence of control — treat capital and control as one transaction, and price the governance rights being handed over.
  • Nominal control on the chart is the one arrangement that is invisible to investors and visible to plaintiffs — ¶ 94 reaches a party through "legal and financial influence," not ownership.
  • Diversification is strong but often unavailable at the scale required; it still does not price the control being sold.
📣

Marketing Lead

The complaint quotes the 2015 promise — "unconstrained by a need to generate financial return," "patents (if any) will be shared with the world" — then pleads that "with the transition to for-profit status… OpenAI also ended its commitment to openness." Your structure is about to change. What do you say about purpose?

  • A Say only what the structure can deliver, and restate it the day the structure changes.
  • B Publish the governance arrangements so the promise and the chart cannot drift apart.
  • C Say less about purpose altogether; let the products speak.
  • D Keep the founding mission language exactly as it is — it is what the brand was built on.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • In a nonprofit or PBC the stated purpose is a charter, not copy — ¶ 56 quotes the founding language verbatim and ¶ 57 builds "Despite its early promises of altruism" on top of it.
  • Keeping the language of a structure you no longer have is how a press release becomes an exhibit.
  • Silence forfeits the recruiting and legitimacy value the form exists to provide — a chartered entity must state a purpose.
⚙️

Operation Lead

The complaint pleads decades of "negotiated licensing agreements" for "narrowly defined purposes," CCC rates of "about ten dollars per article," no permission ever given "for GenAI purposes," and an April 2023 approach that "ha[s] not produced a resolution." How do you obtain rights to your inputs?

  • A Licence before the build, on defined-purpose terms — the only version of this exposure with a ceiling.
  • B Build the provenance system first, so any input can be licensed, excluded or replaced on demand.
  • C Rely on the transformative-use argument and reserve against a court disagreeing.
  • D Take now, negotiate later from a position of strength.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The complaint prices both sides of the choice: a licence at about ten dollars per article with an offer to negotiate, versus disgorgement and a § 503(b) destruction order. A licence is the only form of this exposure with a ceiling.
  • There is no breach-of-contract count — because, as pleaded, no agreement was ever made. The absence of a contract is the case.
  • "Take now, negotiate later" is what actually happened — and the counterparty had a published rate and had asked to negotiate.
💻

Tech Lead

Microsoft trained nothing, yet ¶ 94 pleads it "had the right and ability to prevent OpenAI from using any particular work for training by virtue of its physical control of the supercomputer… and its legal and financial influence." You are the technical partner in a deal like this. How much control do you take?

  • A Decide deliberately — either hold the control and exercise it, or contract it away and stay a utility.
  • B Push the obligation into the contract: data warranties, audit rights, indemnities.
  • C Stay a neutral utility — general-purpose infrastructure, no bespoke build.
  • D Take as much operational control as the partnership allows and rely on the separate entities to hold the liability.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Vicarious liability turns on the right and ability to control plus benefit — so unexercised control is the worst position available: full exposure, zero prevention.
  • Liability attaches to control, not to the org chart — nine Delaware entities are in this caption.
  • Contracts allocate the cost between the parties; they do not stop a plaintiff pleading that you could have said no.
🔒

Security Lead

The prayer for relief seeks "destruction under 17 U.S.C. § 503(b) of all GPT or other LLM models and training sets that incorporate Times Works," alongside disgorgement and a permanent injunction. How do you contain an exposure like that?

  • A Engineer the asset so the exposure is separable — provenance, source removal, retrainable checkpoints.
  • B Contract for it — indemnities, warranties, a duty to defend, so the exposure has an owner.
  • C Reserve and insure, and carry it as a quantified liability.
  • D Hold the models in a separate subsidiary so a judgment cannot reach the parent.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Entity boundaries move money; this remedy is not money. Ask the question that breaks the subsidiary answer: after the order issues, which entity's copy of the weights survives?
  • Provenance, source removal and retrainable checkpoints convert an all-or-nothing order against the asset into a subtraction.
  • Reserving only quantifies what can be quantified — and is silent on what cannot.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question.

After all five leads present their selection + one-sentence defense, rank the five decisions 1–5 by which the company must resolve first, allocate 100 points across them in blocks of 10, and name the corner: which corner of the trilemma — control, capital, or liability protection — did this structure actually buy, which of the other two did it spend, and what would have to have been true for that trade to have been right?

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Rank by where the exposure is created — inputs and exposure belong at the top, and the rank-#1 sentence must cite the complaint.
  • Everything else is downstream of whether input rights were bought or taken: a licence has a ceiling while a § 503(b) order does not.
  • This structure bought capital and paid with control — and the bill arrived as a liability theory built out of the control it sold.

Key takeaway

Legal structure is a trilemma: control, capital and liability protection — every choice buys one by spending another. Boundaries allocate money losses; they do not answer who was in control.

Nine entities did not keep anyone out. Control put them in the caption.

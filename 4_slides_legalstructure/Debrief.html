Use after discussion. These takeaways explain the reasoning; compare them with your original decision.

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Capital comes with terms. Identify the decision rights, dependencies and financial obligations attached to your proposed funding source.
  • Use all three corners. Explain how the proposal affects control, access to capital and exposure, including the questions the packet leaves unresolved.
  • Test the arrangement under disagreement. Identify who could refuse a proposed action and what would happen if the partners wanted different things.

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Promises and governance must be examined together. Identify what the proposed statement commits the organization to and who has authority to deliver it.
  • Distinguish the documents. Compare public messaging, governance arrangements and the purposes described in the packet without treating them as interchangeable.
  • Plan for change. Explain how the team would review its statements when its funding, structure or activities change.

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Inputs carry terms as well as costs. Identify what your proposed approach requires the company to know about an input and its permitted uses.
  • Compare the tradeoffs. Explain how each relevant cost, restriction and uncertainty affects control, capital and exposure under your proposal.
  • Separate the claim from the outcome. Distinguish the complaint’s allegations and requested relief from what has been established in the supplied material.

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Control has a practical meaning. Map who designs, operates, monitors and can stop the system, then compare those roles with the agreements.
  • Assess the chosen boundary. Explain how your proposal changes capability, dependence and responsibility between the partners.
  • Specify evidence and escalation. Identify what information a decision-maker needs and who acts when the partnership encounters a disputed input or use.

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Explain how capital, control, and responsibility fit together; an organization chart alone is not an argument.
  • Different rankings can be defensible. Connect the priorities to evidence and the weekly concept.
  • Example allocation: 40/30/20/10 totals 100. Explain your own allocation; priority points are not course-grade points.

Key takeaway

  • Use the Structure Trilemma to examine the arrangements in the case: who funds, who decides and who faces which consequences. Support the analysis with the supplied complaint while keeping allegations and legal outcomes distinct.
  • Which rights and responsibilities would your arrangement create in practice?

The lines between them are the course

  • Change who the customer is and your pricing model breaks.
  • Change your legal structure and your funding options change with it.
  • Win on technology and you have made your IP position both more valuable and more attacked.
  • Cut the budget and you have quietly changed your deployment timing.

Entrepreneurship is not about knowing one answer. It is about making interconnected decisions under uncertainty — and knowing which decision you would have to unwind first.

Reflection questions

  • Which framework changed how you look at a business you had already made up your mind about?
  • Which simulation decision was hardest — and was it the data or the room?
  • Where is your venture's evidence thinnest, and are you avoiding it?
  • What has to improve before the final presentation?
  • What did you learn about yourself as a teammate?

Funding, honestly

At your stage the money that matters is small, non-dilutive, and attached to a program: competitions, grants, and fellowships.

Nobody writes a term sheet off a class project. What they fund is evidence — and evidence is what your team has been assembling since Week 1.

Your personal project portfolio is the 10% that outlives the course. It is the artifact you take to an interview next fall. Make sure it says what you decided and why, not just what you built.

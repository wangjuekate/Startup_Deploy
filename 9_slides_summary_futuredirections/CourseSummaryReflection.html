The toolkit / Eight mental models

The semester in one picture

01 · Value-Chain Chokepoint

Find the step that serves the customer’s real job, is hard to copy and fails visibly. Protect the capability that earns trust and bargaining power.

02 · Take-Rate Waterfall

Trace volume → revenue → contribution → profit. Fix costs that grow with each sale before scaling; volume does not cure a per-unit loss.

03 · Appropriability Matrix

Ask whether disclosure destroys the advantage and whether copying can be detected. Match protection to the asset, including people and know-how.

04 · Structure Trilemma

Weigh control, capital and liability together. Ask who decides, who funds the venture and who bears the consequences when something goes wrong.

05 · Dominant-Design Clock

Identify the stage for each product: explore before dominance, build adoption during the battle, then operate efficiently and find the next source of value.

06 · Evidence → Choice → Deployment

Study competitors, discover pain, position the venture, decide what to keep or change, then deploy and test. Feed pilot evidence back into the next decision.

07 · Value-Metric Triangle

Choose what to charge for, the price level and who pays differently. Test whether the billing metric grows with customer value — and what efficiency does to revenue.

08 · Reversibility Budget

Sort spending into committed, sticky and flexible. Compare unavoidable payments with downside revenue; identify the time and cost needed to stop spending.

Summaries follow the eight concept lectures. Module 6 now uses the competitive deployment process: evidence → choice → deployment. Treat the IP and structure models as prompts for investigation, not automatic legal conclusions.

Apply / From discovery to deployment

A roadmap to deploying your venture

  1. Find the value

    Define the customer’s job and the capability that makes your offering worth choosing.

    Value-Chain Chokepoint

  2. Make delivery work

    Map how value is delivered and what remains after each sale’s variable costs.

    Take-Rate Waterfall

  3. Protect and organize

    Identify the assets to protect and align decision rights, funding and responsibility.

    Appropriability · Structure Trilemma

  4. Choose your entry

    Read the market’s design stage, compare alternatives and test why customers would switch.

    Dominant Design · Competitive Deployment

  5. Price the value

    Choose the billing basis, price and segments. Connect adoption and usage to revenue.

    Value-Metric Triangle

  6. Fund the next milestone

    Build the budget, stress-test lower revenue and preserve room to change course.

    Reversibility Budget

At every step: gather evidence → decide → test → revise.

Revisit earlier steps as evidence changes. Pricing can expose an operating problem; a budget constraint can change the entry sequence; an IP dependency can change what you build or obtain through partners.

Connect / One venture, interdependent decisions

A change in one choice travels through the plan

Customer → pricing

A different customer can change the value metric, buying process and willingness to pay.

Technology → operations

A new capability can change suppliers, permissions, delivery costs and deployment timing.

Pricing → budget

The billing basis and sales assumptions must produce the revenue shown in the budget.

Budget → market entry

Less funding can change the first segment, launch scope and milestone you can reach.

When one assumption changes, update every decision that depends on it.

Apply / Final venture proposal and pitch

Turn the models into one coherent proposal

A · Venture and program fit

Define the customer problem, selected support program, eligibility, request and milestone. Include the application preparation appendix.

B · Market entry

Choose the first segment and launch approach. Explain dominant design, standards, complements and adoption barriers.

C · Technology and competition

Specify what to build, acquire or obtain through partners. Compare alternatives and explain differentiation and dependencies.

D · Pricing

State the price and billing basis. Compare alternatives and connect adoption, volume and margins to the budget.

E · Financial budgeting

Show a base budget and revenue 20% below it. Classify spending and explain stopping costs and your response.

Check the whole proposal

Keep claims, prices, sales assumptions, milestones and support requests consistent across the report and video.

Sections A–E are each worth 20 points per deliverable. Use the rubric for your team or individual option in the final assignment guide.

Apple v. OpenAI — A Secret and a Contract

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, September 24

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • Every statement of fact must begin "the complaint alleges" — hold each other to it out loud.
  • As a group, defend your decision and name the IP instrument it relies on.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and defense.
💰

Financial Lead

Apple pleads "hundreds of billions of dollars and decades of effort" invested in information it keeps confidential — and "no adequate remedy at law." Where does the next protection dollar go?

  • A Into the operational system that keeps secrecy intact — access, offboarding, chain of custody.
  • B Into the enforcement action; a credible case makes every other protection believable.
  • C Into patenting more of what can be patented.
  • D Treat it as a cost of doing business — more than 400 former employees now work there.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • "No adequate remedy at law" is a concession: damages cannot put the secret back. For information whose value dies on disclosure, only spending that stops it leaving preserves the asset.
  • A visible, expensive case buys deterrence — it makes the IPA and the NDAs mean something to the next employee — not the asset back.
  • Patenting the unreleased designs would publish them; negative know-how ("what approaches were tested and rejected") cannot be patented at all.
📣

Marketing Lead

The alleged secrets include "proprietary product designs for current and future unreleased products" and "confidential product roadmaps, milestone schedules." What is the communications posture?

  • A Say nothing about the products, everything about the consequences.
  • B Make the protection programme visible to candidates, partners and suppliers.
  • C Say as little as possible about any of it, including the litigation.
  • D Pre-announce roadmaps to establish public priority.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • For unreleased hardware, the surprise and the secret are the same asset — nothing about the product can be said, while publicising consequences costs nothing and raises the price of taking the information.
  • People comply with rules they know exist and have seen enforced — visibility to candidates and suppliers is prevention rather than remedy.
  • Publishing roadmaps to establish priority is how patents work, not how secrets work — it destroys the asset being protected and hands the competitor the plan.
⚙️

Operation Lead

The complaint alleges a partner "carr[ied] out a specific trade secret metal-finishing technique for OpenAI, misleading the partner to believe they had Apple's permission." Suppliers already sign NDAs and follow chain-of-custody protocols. Where do you act?

  • A Fix authorisation at the partner boundary — verified requests, named contacts, purpose limits.
  • B Extend chain of custody and audit to every partner holding our equipment or processes.
  • C Narrow the circle — fewer partners see any given process.
  • D Restrict which suppliers may work with AI companies at all.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The partner allegedly did not break confidentiality — he complied with what he believed was authorised. That is an authorisation failure, not a confidentiality failure.
  • NDAs govern what a partner may disclose; nothing in the pleaded control set tells him how to verify who is asking.
  • Banning suppliers from working with AI companies addresses who a partner works for, not how the partner checks the request — and pushes suppliers away.
💻

Tech Lead

The complaint's allegations come from "Apple's company-owned devices and systems" — messages on work laptops, repository access, downloads. Apple says it has "virtually no visibility" into what happened afterwards. Where does engineering effort go?

  • A Instrument the exfiltration paths — access logging, departure anomaly detection, device custody.
  • B Close the authentication-bug class, unreturned devices, and access surviving departure.
  • C Harden device management — encryption, remote wipe, blocking personal email and removable media.
  • D Lock the environment down so files cannot be moved at all.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Every allegation with a chance of being proved came from Apple's own logs, devices and messages — detection is the load-bearing control; it converts a loss into a case.
  • The pleaded vectors were specific: an authentication bug, an unreturned laptop, a colleague's still-authenticated machine.
  • Absolute prevention is unachievable — engineers who cannot move files cannot design hardware — and the pleaded evidence came from observing, not from preventing.
🔒

Security Lead

The complaint alleges departing employees emailed files to personal accounts, that a "Need to Know" document describing departure-security procedures was shared with new hires "before they give notice," and "a pattern by employees who depart for OpenAI of taking steps to evade the security processes." Where do you set the bar?

  • A Treat departure as a monitored lifecycle event, not a last-day checklist.
  • B Fix inbound as well as outbound — so we never receive another company's secrets either.
  • C Strengthen training and the annual Business Conduct course.
  • D Rely on the agreements; every employee signs the IPA and it survives termination.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The alleged conduct starts before notice (a "Need to Know" document shared with new hires) and continues after the badge is returned — a last-day checklist is invisible to both ends of that window.
  • Ask what the IPA detected: every individual named allegedly signed one. A contract is a remedy that operates after detection.
  • Receiving another firm's secrets acquires their liability — fix inbound as well as outbound.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question.

After all five leads present their selection + one-sentence defense, rank the five decisions 1–5 by which the company must resolve first, allocate 100 points across them in blocks of 10, and name the instrument: which IP tool is this company relying on, what does that instrument cost, and what would have to change for a different one to be correct?

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Rank by where a secret actually leaves from — departures and partners belong at the top, and the rank-#1 sentence must cite the complaint.
  • A trade secret can only be taken by someone who was trusted — the perimeter that failed was human, not technical.
  • The instrument is trade secrecy: its price is that it fails permanently, and only ever to people who were trusted.

Key takeaway

Choose an IP instrument by detection, advantage life and disclosure cost — and remember that trade-secret protection is an operational system, not a legal one. It cannot be bought or filed; it has to be run.

A patent is a bargain. A secret is an operating system.

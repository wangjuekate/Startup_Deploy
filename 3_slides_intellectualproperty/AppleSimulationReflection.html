Apple v. OpenAI — A Secret and a Contract

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, September 24

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • Describe contested conduct as "the complaint alleges". Distinguish allegations, evidence and requested remedies from established findings.
  • As a group, defend your decision and name the IP instrument it relies on.
  • Cite a specific detail from the complaint, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and reasoning.
💰

Financial Lead

Apple pleads it has "no adequate remedy at law." Where does the next protection dollar go?

  • A Treat it as a cost of doing business. Over four hundred former employees now work there; we cannot police where people go or what they remember.
  • B Into patenting more of what can be patented. A patent is at least enforceable without having to prove somebody took something.
  • C Into the enforcement action. A credible, expensive, public case is what makes every contractual protection we have believable to the next employee and the next partner.
  • D Into the operational system that keeps secrecy intact — need-to-know access, offboarding, supplier chain of custody. For information whose value dies on disclosure, the lawsuit begins after we have already lost the asset.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Identify what the spending protects. Distinguish the value of an asset from the cost of detecting, preventing or responding to its loss. Explain which objective your allocation serves.
  • Apply the matrix before choosing an instrument. Consider detectability, the life of the advantage and the cost of disclosure for the information at issue.
  • Make the tradeoff explicit. State what your proposal leaves less protected and what evidence would make you reallocate the next dollar.
📣

Marketing Lead

When the secret and the surprise are the same asset, what does the company say — and when?

  • A Say as little as possible about any of it, including the litigation. Attention on the case is attention on what was taken.
  • B Say nothing about the products and everything about the consequences. For unreleased hardware the surprise IS the asset; what we can publicise for free is what happens to people who take it.
  • C Make the protection programme visible to candidates, partners and suppliers — people comply with rules they know exist and have seen enforced.
  • D Pre-announce roadmaps and milestones to establish public priority, so nobody can later claim they got there first.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Communication changes the information available. Identify the audience, purpose and timing of your proposed message, and what it would reveal.
  • Connect the message to the asset. Use the matrix to explain how disclosure affects the source of advantage and the ability to demonstrate misuse.
  • Separate allegations from conclusions. Identify what the complaint claims, what supports the claim in the packet and what remains unresolved.
⚙️

Operation Lead

Your secrets live at companies you do not control. Where do you act?

  • A Extend chain of custody and audit to every partner holding our equipment or processes, not only to the components and the scrap.
  • B Fix authorisation at the partner boundary — verified requests, named authorised contacts and purpose limits — because the pleaded failure was a partner who believed the instruction came from us.
  • C Narrow the circle — fewer partners see any given process, and no partner sees enough of the system to reconstruct it.
  • D Restrict which suppliers may work with AI companies at all, as a condition of doing business with us.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Protection crosses company boundaries. Map who holds the information, who can request it and who can authorize its use.
  • Consider the cost of each control. Explain how your proposal affects collaboration, supplier capacity and the ability to trace a disputed transfer.
  • Test the process in practice. Describe a scenario that would challenge the proposed arrangement and what evidence would show whether it worked.
💻

Tech Lead

A secret whose departure you cannot prove is a secret you cannot enforce. Where does engineering effort go?

  • A Instrument the exfiltration paths — repository access logging, anomaly detection around departures, device custody — because every provable allegation in this complaint came from our own systems.
  • B Close the specific hole and the class of holes like it: the authentication bug, unreturned devices, and access that survives a departure.
  • C Lock the environment down so tightly that files cannot be moved or copied at all, and accept the friction as the cost of secrecy.
  • D Harden device management — full-disk encryption, remote wipe, blocking personal email and removable media on work machines.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Technical evidence serves a question. Specify what your system would need to establish about access, copying or transfer, and what it could not establish.
  • Balance protection and productive work. Explain the effect of your proposal on legitimate engineering activity as well as on the risk being addressed.
  • Connect detection to the matrix. Describe how the ability to observe and document an event affects the usefulness of the instrument your team chooses.
🔒

Security Lead

The alleged conduct happens before notice and continues after the badge is returned. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Rely on the agreements. Every employee signs the IPA, it survives termination, and it is enforceable in court.
  • B Treat departure as a monitored lifecycle event, not a last-day checklist. The alleged conduct happens before notice is given and continues after the badge is returned.
  • C Fix inbound as well as outbound — interview and onboarding protocols so that we never receive another company's confidential information either.
  • D Strengthen training and the annual Business Conduct course so obligations are understood rather than merely signed.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Information moves across a lifecycle. Consider hiring, ongoing work, changing roles and departure when defining the scope of your proposal.
  • Responsibilities need owners. Explain who implements, checks and responds to the controls, including when work involves another company.
  • Evaluate the remaining exposure. State what behavior your plan would address, what remains uncertain and what finding would require a different response.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After the five leads present, consider budget, disclosure, partners, detection, and departures.

  • 1 Rank the problems 1–5 by what must be resolved first for a company whose most valuable information is unpublished. Use each once. Justify #1 in one sentence citing a specific detail from the complaint.
  • 2 Allocate 100 points across the five problems, totaling exactly 100.
  • 3 Name the instrument. Patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or contract? Explain what the chosen instrument costs and what would have to change for another to fit.
🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Choose from the characteristics of the asset. Explain the instrument, its costs and the conditions under which another instrument would fit better.
  • Priorities require evidence. Ranking sets order; weighting expresses relative emphasis. Support both with specific material from the complaint.
  • Evaluate the combined protection plan. Explain how budget, disclosure, partner practices, detection and employee processes interact without treating allegations as findings.

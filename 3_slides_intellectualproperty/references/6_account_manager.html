Account Manager — Case Overview

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Apple Inc. v. Liu, Tan, OpenAI Foundation, OpenAI Group PBC and io Products, LLC, No. 5:26-cv-07078 (N.D. Cal.), complaint filed July 10, 2026

⚠️ Everything on this page is an allegation from a filed complaint. Nothing has been proved and no court has ruled. Say “the complaint alleges” every time you state a fact — and hold your four leads to it too. That discipline is graded, and you are the one who enforces it in the room.

You answer no functional question of your own. The other four leads each own one decision and one page. You own the whole case — and the order in which its problems get solved. Your page is the overview. Read it before they present.

What kind of document this is

Not a filing. Not a ruling. A complaint — the document one side files to start a lawsuit. It is written by the plaintiff’s lawyers to persuade. Everything in it is an accusation.

“COMPLAINT FOR TRADE SECRET MISAPPROPRIATION AND BREACH OF CONTRACT” — jury trial demanded. Filed July 10, 2026, U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California, San Jose Division. 41 pages.

“This case is about Apple’s former employees stealing Apple’s trade secrets for the benefit of OpenAI.”

— ¶ 1

Note what Apple did not sue over. Not a patent. Not a copyright. Not a trademark. A secret, and a contract. That single fact is the module. Ask your leads about it.

The parties, as pleaded

Party What the complaint alleges
Apple Inc. Plaintiff. “Apple surprises and delights its customers each year… keeping much of its work secret until the moment its finished products are ready to be revealed.” (¶ 2)
Chang Liu “over eight years” at Apple, Senior System Electrical Engineer for iPhone. Left for OpenAI January 2026. Signed Apple’s IPA.
Tang Yew Tan “twenty-four years at Apple, most recently as Vice President of Product Design for iPhone and Apple Watch. Today he is OpenAI’s Chief Hardware Officer.” Signed Apple’s IPA.
OpenAI Foundation Delaware nonprofit.
OpenAI Group PBC Delaware public benefit corporation.
io Products, LLC Co-founded 2024 by Mr. Tan and other ex-Apple executives as “the dedicated hardware vehicle for OpenAI”; acquired by OpenAI May 2025 for ≈ $6.5 billion. Pleaded as “an alter ego of OpenAI.”

Legal basis: the Defend Trade Secrets Act, 18 U.S.C. §§ 1836 et seq., plus breach of Apple’s Intellectual Property Agreement (IPA) against the two individuals. Six claims for relief.

The whole case on one page

What Apple says its secrets are — five pleaded categories, ¶ 40(a)–(e), each said to “derive independent economic value from its secrecy”:

# Category The line that matters
a Hardware engineering & product design “designs for current and future unreleased products… confidential product roadmaps, milestone schedules”
b Manufacturing & industrial design “proprietary metal alloys, metal-finishing techniques… proprietary equipment housed at supplier facilities”
c Component technologies “the identities of the specialized sub-suppliers and vendors”
d Testing & validation methods “the negative know-how of what approaches have been tested, rejected, or refined”
e Supply chain & business operations “Apple’s systems-level integration knowledge… is a trade secret in its own right”

The value claim: “Apple has invested hundreds of billions of dollars and decades of effort… A competitor with access to it could bypass years of independent research and development, skip the capital expenditure… and bring products to market faster and at lower cost.” (¶ 41)

The measures pleaded (what the law calls reasonable measures): the IPA signed by every employee · supplier NDAs before access, “and even then only… on a need-to-know basis” · annual Business Conduct training · departure procedures and device/badge return · Access Manager provisioning, encryption, device authentication · “locked buildings, security cameras, guards” · project code names, supplier ID screening, “chain-of-custody protocols… secure disposal of scrap materials”, a proprietary prototype shipping process. (¶¶ 44–51)

What is alleged to have gone wrong:

Route Allegation
Devices An unreturned laptop; “I still have another computer”; use of a colleague’s still-authenticated machine (¶¶ 55–56)
A bug Access to network storage survived departure via “a then-unknown authentication vulnerability” — “LOL, I found out I can access the [network storage], so funny.” Not reported (¶ 57)
Files “dozens of confidential files” downloaded, including “over a thousand pages” of technical work (¶¶ 58–59)
A colleague Months of “frequent updates on Apple’s active confidential projects, vendor decisions, and engineering details” from someone still inside (¶ 62); coaching on how to copy files “to avoid trouble with the security team” (¶ 63)
Interviews Candidates still at Apple allegedly told to bring “Actual parts” for “show and tell”; internal code names used to ask “What’s the plan[?]” about unannounced products
A partner A trusted partner allegedly performed Apple’s “trade secret metal-finishing technique” for OpenAI, “misled… to believe they had Apple’s permission” (¶¶ 80–81)
Exits An Apple “Need to Know” document on departure security allegedly circulated to new hires before they gave notice; advice to avoid the “dreaded walk out” and to “won’t sign anything at the exit interview” (¶ 84–85)

What Apple wants: damages plus exemplary damages under 18 U.S.C. § 1836(b)(3)(C) for conduct alleged “willful and malicious”; injunctions to preserve evidence, to stop “further acquiring, possessing, using, or disclosing”, and to return all copies.

“Apple has no adequate remedy at law.” — ¶ 124

Two facts to keep in your pocket: - “Over four hundred former Apple employees now work at OpenAI.” - “Apple wrote to OpenAI to raise its concerns… OpenAI never responded.” (¶ 14) - The ChatGPT/Apple Intelligence agreement “is not at issue here.” (¶ 53 n.13) — somebody will try to make the case about that. It isn’t.

Your task, in three parts

Part 1 — Rank them 1 to 4

Put the four problems in the order they must be resolved for a company whose most valuable information is unpublished, from the one that has to be fixed first down to the one that can wait longest. Each problem is used exactly once. Then give one sentence justifying rank #1 — and that sentence must cite something specific from the complaint.

Part 3 — Name the instrument

Out loud, to the team: which instrument is this company actually relying on for its most valuable information — patent, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or contract? What does that instrument cost it? And what would have to change for a different one to be correct?

How to run the room

  • Rank by where a secret actually leaves from, not by who argued hardest.
  • Enforce the word “alleges.” A lead who states an allegation as fact gets corrected in the room. You are graded on this too.
  • Watch for the litigation story. Somebody will treat the lawsuit as the solution. Read ¶ 124 back to them: no adequate remedy at law is Apple conceding that money cannot put a secret back. A case is what you do after you have already lost the asset.
  • Watch for “patent it.” Ask what a patent application does to a design for an unreleased product. Publishing is how patents work; silence is how secrets work. You cannot run both on the same asset.
  • Watch for “stop them going to competitors.” California generally does not enforce non-competes — and note the complaint never asks for one.
  • Watch for the ChatGPT deal. ¶ 53 n.13 takes it off the table deliberately. Do not let the room relitigate it.
  • You are allowed to disagree with your own team. The ranking is yours to sign.

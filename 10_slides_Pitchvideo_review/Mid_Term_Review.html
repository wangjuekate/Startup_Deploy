02 / What worked well

Five strengths to preserve

  1. Engaging, hands-on learning

    Keep simulations that make venture-planning concepts tangible through realistic business decisions.

  2. Collaboration and participation

    Preserve teamwork and discussions that encourage different perspectives, shared decisions, and learning from mistakes.

  3. Positive, approachable teaching

    Continue bringing energy, enthusiasm, and knowledge—and walking around to answer questions.

  4. Helpful debriefs

    Keep post-simulation discussions and weekly reviews that explain decisions and reinforce concepts.

  5. Accessible learning environment

    Preserve a manageable, welcoming course where students can participate without prior experience.

03 / Improvement 1 of 5 • Clearer instructions • Problem

Expectations and navigation need to be clearer.

What students told us

The most recurring concern was unclear assignment expectations, simulation directions, SkillSimm navigation, and midterm requirements. Students wanted concise, consistent, easily accessible guidance.

03 / Improvement 1 of 5 • Clearer instructions • Solution

Make the instructions easier to use

01

Planned · Demonstrate the platform

Provide an instructional video showing how to use the simulation.

02

Planned · Explain expectations and grading

Add instructions about assignment expectations and grading details.

03

Suggested · Keep guidance together

Place the video, assignment checklist, and grading criteria in one consistent location.

03 / Improvement 2 of 5 • Preparation before simulations • Problem

Students need concepts before decisions.

What students told us

Some students felt confused early in the semester or entered simulations before understanding the concepts. They asked for slower explanations, better orientation, and explicit connections to prior lessons.

03 / Improvement 2 of 5 • Preparation before simulations • Solution

Orient students, then guide each team

01

Planned · Check the starting point

Use a pre-survey for the first simulation to understand team needs.

02

Planned · Track learning after each simulation

Use a post-survey after every simulation to make team guidance more specific.

03

Suggested · Start with a concept walkthrough

Slow down the introduction, connect the activity to prior lessons, and demonstrate a decision before teams begin.

03 / Improvement 3 of 5 • Variety and class time • Problem

The weekly format can feel repetitive.

What students told us

Although simulations were a major strength, several students wanted occasional lectures, additional startup cases, other activities, and more productive use of time after finishing simulations.

03 / Improvement 3 of 5 • Variety and class time • Solution

Keep simulations; vary the surrounding work

01

Suggested · Rotate the opening

Alternate a short concept lesson, a startup case, and a focused discussion before the simulation.

02

Suggested · Give early finishers a useful task

Ask teams to compare a different decision or connect their result to a real startup.

03

Suggested · Close with the learning purpose

Use the debrief to explain what this week adds to prior activities.

03 / Improvement 4 of 5 • Technical reliability and access • Problem

Technical friction interrupts learning.

What students told us

Lag, glitches, inconsistent instructions, and difficulty accessing simulations afterward interfered with learning and reflection assignments.

03 / Improvement 4 of 5 • Technical reliability and access • Solution

Check access and preserve the learning record

01

Planned · Show the access workflow

Use the instructional video to explain how to navigate the simulation.

02

Suggested · Check and track failures

Check access before class; record the step and error when a problem occurs so it can be reproduced and fixed.

03

Suggested · Make reflection materials available

Provide a stable record of the scenario, key decisions, and outcomes for students to revisit afterward.

03 / Improvement 5 of 5 • Materials and feedback • Problem

Materials need structure; feedback needs specifics.

What students told us

Some students requested better-organized materials and more specific feedback. One student explicitly found the heavy use of AI-generated readings fatiguing and the feedback too broad.

03 / Improvement 5 of 5 • Materials and feedback • Solution

Make support more focused and actionable

01

Use office hours for specific feedback

Offer more specific feedback through office hours.

02

Use surveys to direct support

Use team-performance information from simulation surveys to target guidance.

03

Curate materials and feedback

Organize readings around each activity, vary sources with selected startup cases, and identify a concrete next step in feedback.

04 / Next steps

A consistent team and support routine

01

Keep the midterm teams

Use the same teams for the next four simulations and the final project.

02

Track progress and target guidance

Use a pre-survey for the first simulation and a post-survey after each simulation to understand team performance.

03

Make help easier to use

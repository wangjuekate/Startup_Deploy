Stage 1 — Key takeaways

Locate each product on the clock

Dominant-Design Clock: Diagnose the gate for each product line separately.

  • One company, different gates. In this simulation, the car and connector are at Gate 3; autonomy is at Gate 1, where the architecture is still being decided.
  • Map the complements. Autonomy depends on fleet charging; network expansion depends on partners; customer promises depend on engineering and operations.
  • Pool evidence before choosing. Each executive holds a different constraint. The shared capital ceiling covers engineering, demand, and the network together.

  • A. Spread the budget across autonomy, cells and Optimus to keep every bet alive.
  • B. Concentrate on autonomy, coordinated with fleet charging from the network and within the capital ceiling.
  • C. Concentrate on in-house cells and the next-generation platform.
  • D. Slow AI spending until Robotaxi proves it can make money.

Stage 2 — Key takeaways

Match the investment to the gate

Dominant-Design Clock: After a design settles, identify where value moves.

  • Gate 3: earn from the position. An adopted connector creates an opportunity to earn from charging operations, software, and services. Standard adoption alone does not guarantee profit.
  • Distinguish access from value capture. Keeping the connector and network open can support paid usage. Partners fund hardware while Tesla retains operating responsibilities; open access does not mean free service.
  • Gate 1: fund a bounded commitment. Under this round’s assumptions, coordinate autonomy with charging and demand within the ceiling. Choosing autonomy here does not establish a universal rule to commit everything to one uncertain architecture.

  • Capital — the CFO’s question
  • Demand — the CMO’s question
  • The network — the COO’s question
  • Engineering — the CTO’s question

Rank from 1–4; use each choice once.

Stage 3 — Set Company Priorities

Chief Executive Officer (CEO)

Why should your first-ranked question take priority for the company? Refer to information an executive shared.

Stage 3 — Key takeaways

Set priorities across gates

Dominant-Design Clock: Support the next architecture while earning from the settled one.

  • Rank commitments and dependencies together. Engineering defines the autonomy effort; capital limits the whole plan; the network enables fleet charging; demand must promise only what can be delivered.
  • Make the company case. Explain the first priority using evidence shared by another executive, the relevant gate, and the trade-off for the rest of the team.
  • Define when to revisit the ranking. Changes in autonomy readiness, charging capacity, partner participation, or available capital should trigger review. Check whether the strategy still fits the product’s gate.

Key takeaway

Apply the Dominant-Design Clock to each product line. Explain what is still being decided, what adoption depends on and how the company expects to capture value as the market changes.

Tesla at Three Gates

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, October 22

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • As a group, name the gate (1, 2 or 3) your decision belongs to and what strategy that gate calls for.
  • Cite a figure from your packet, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and reasoning.
💰

Financial Lead

Where does the next dollar of capital go?

  • A Pull back. Revenue fell $2.86 billion and profit fell $3.30 billion — cut R&D and capex, protect the $3.79 billion, and reinvest when the market recovers.
  • B Follow the open gates — energy storage and the AI/autonomy programs. Energy grew 27% at a 29.8% margin while automotive fell 10% at 17.8%; capital belongs where the architecture is still being decided.
  • C Fund cost reduction in automotive. It is still $69.5 billion of revenue and 1.64 million deliveries — a point of margin there is worth more than either new business is worth in total.
  • D Concentrate on autonomy specifically. R&D is already up 41% to $6.4 billion and Robotaxi is the only thing that changes what a vehicle is worth after the vehicle itself is a commodity.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Allocate by a stated strategic hypothesis. Name the product line and gate your proposal concerns, and explain the evidence for that placement.
  • Compare both returns and commitments. Consider current economics, development uncertainty and how much flexibility the investment leaves.
  • Define a review point. Identify a market or technical signal that would change the allocation rather than assuming today's gate remains unchanged.
📣

Marketing Lead

The tax credit is gone, deliveries fell 8%, and margin has fallen three years running. How do you sell?

  • A Cut price again to defend volume. 1.64 million deliveries is the number the market watches, and we can make it back on scale.
  • B Sell the network and the software rather than the sticker price — paid Supercharging, insurance and services grew 19%, and they are the part a competitor's car cannot come with.
  • C Sell affordability head-on with financing and lease terms rather than price cuts. The credit is gone and monthly payment, not sticker price, is what the buyer actually decides on.
  • D Sell the future — market Robotaxi and FSD to make the car the cheapest way into an autonomous fleet.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Positioning depends on the competitive stage. Explain what the customer is choosing between and how your proposed message fits that choice.
  • Separate demand from value capture. State how the plan could affect volume, willingness to pay, retention and margin.
  • Test the promise. Identify evidence needed to support the proposed positioning and what would make you revise it.
⚙️

Operation Lead

Every major automaker has adopted the connector. What is the network for now?

  • A Aim the build-out at Robotaxi corridors first — the fleet needs dense, reliable charging more than any individual owner does.
  • B Scale it through Supercharger for Business — let third parties buy, install and price the hardware while we keep the software, network operations, maintenance and driver support.
  • C Keep it open to every automaker and keep operating it ourselves — a standard only pays if everyone uses it, and running the network is what turns other companies' cars into our revenue.
  • D Now that NACS has won, re-prioritise the network for Tesla owners and Robotaxi. We gave the connector away and got nothing back for it.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Adoption and operation are different questions. Explain how your proposal affects who uses the network, who funds it and who earns from its use.
  • Trace the role of complements. Identify the partners, infrastructure and services needed for the chosen network strategy to work.
  • Evaluate the next stage. State which change in the market would justify moving the dial on access, investment or operating responsibility.
💻

Tech Lead

R&D is $6.4 billion and rose 41% in a year revenue fell. Where does the engineering go?

  • A Concentrate on autonomy. Every vehicle delivered is already designed for it, so the fleet is the data asset — and the next architecture is being decided in software, not in sheet metal.
  • B Spread the budget across autonomy, cells and Optimus. Nobody knows which architecture wins, so keep all three bets alive.
  • C Slow the AI spending until Robotaxi proves it can make money. R&D rose 41% to $6.4 billion in a year when revenue fell.
  • D Concentrate on in-house cells and the next-generation platform. Cost per vehicle is what keeps us in the market long enough for the software fight to matter.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Engineering follows a gate-specific question. Identify what remains technically or commercially unresolved in the product line you prioritize.
  • Compare concentration with optionality. Explain what the chosen scope enables and which alternatives it leaves less developed.
  • Set evidence for the next commitment. Describe a milestone and a finding that would make the team redirect engineering effort.
🔒

Security Lead

A fleet that updates itself over the air is now also a fleet that drives itself. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Rank by severity after launch. Get Robotaxi into more cities first and harden the service as real incidents come in.
  • B Treat vehicle and product security as safety-critical and fleet-wide — the over-the-air channel reaches every car at once, so an exploited update or a Robotaxi incident is a regulatory event for the whole company, not a support ticket.
  • C Concentrate on Robotaxi. It is the newest attack surface, the least proven, and the only one where nobody is in the driver's seat.
  • D Concentrate on customer data and privacy compliance across every market we operate in — the regulatory exposure is broad and continuous.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Deployment stage and consequence both matter. Consider uncertainty about adoption alongside the potential effects of a failure when assessing the proposal.
  • Map how a failure could spread. Identify the relevant product, software, data and operating dependencies without assuming every risk has the same scope.
  • Specify readiness and response. Explain the evidence needed before expansion, who can pause deployment and how the team would learn from a concern.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After the five leads present, consider capital, the network, safety and security, demand, and engineering.

  • 1 Rank the problems 1–5 by what must be resolved to remain the company others build around in ten years. Use each once. Justify #1 in one sentence citing a figure from your packet.
  • 2 Allocate 100 points across the five problems in blocks of 10, totaling exactly 100.
  • 3 Name the gates. For cars, the charging network, energy, and autonomy / Robotaxi, identify Gate 1 (pre-dominance), Gate 2 (the battle), or Gate 3 (settled), and the strategy it calls for.
🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Place products separately. Explain the gate for each business using the packet rather than assigning one stage to the whole company.
  • Connect priorities to the ten-year objective. Ranking sets order; weighting expresses relative emphasis. Describe how the five decisions shape future adoption and value capture.
  • Look for a changing clock. Identify evidence that would move a business to another gate and explain how the strategy would change.

Key takeaway

Apply the Dominant-Design Clock to each product line. Explain what is still being decided, what adoption depends on and how the company expects to capture value as the market changes.

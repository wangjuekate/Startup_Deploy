Account Manager — Company Overview

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Tesla, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You answer no functional question of your own. The other four leads each own one decision and one page. You own the whole company — and the order in which its problems get solved. Your page is the overview. Read it before they present.

The company, in the filing’s own terms

“We are focused on bringing artificial intelligence into the real world, through products and services like Full Self-Driving (‘FSD’) (Supervised) and Robotaxi, as well as working to develop and commercialize AI robots (including Optimus). We intend to leverage our current operations, in which we design, develop, manufacture, sell and lease high-performance fully electric vehicles and energy generation and storage systems… to achieve that objective.”

— Item 1, Business

Two reportable segments: (i) automotive — including services, paid Supercharging, insurance, used vehicles — and (ii) energy generation and storage — Powerwall, Megapack, solar.

Three things happened in 2025 that you need to hold at once: 1. Every major automaker adopted Tesla’s charging connector (NACS). 2. Car revenue fell 10% and automotive margin fell for the third straight year. 3. Research spending rose 41% chasing autonomy, and Robotaxi launched in June.

The standard: “We offer Supercharger access to non-Tesla vehicles… with all major automakers announcing their adoption of the North American Charging Standard (NACS) in certain markets.” Under Supercharger for Business, third parties “purchase and install Superchargers and set the charging price, while Tesla remains responsible for software updates, network operations, maintenance and driver support.” Services growth came “primarily due to increases in paid Supercharging sessions” and insurance and maintenance.

The next architecture: Robotaxi launched June 2025; “every Tesla vehicle delivered today is designed for autonomy”; Cybercab to follow; Optimus in development. Megapack is positioned against “AI infrastructure driv[ing] rapid load growth.”

The policy shock: OBBBA removed the EV tax credit and “restricted certain regulatory credit programs” — credit revenue fell 28%. Tariffs hit the energy business harder than automotive.

Competition: the car market “will become even more competitive”; the company notes that competitors’ entries “promote the development of the electric vehicle market.” In autonomy it competes “along with traditional ride-hailing and taxi services.”

Security: risks include “harm to employees or customers”; incidents are “ranked by severity”; every vehicle is updated over the air.

How to run the room

  • Rank by which gate the problem belongs to, not by volume of voice.
  • You may not rank #1 anything a lead could not defend with a number.
  • Watch for the failure story. Somebody will read a 17.8% automotive margin as bad execution. Ask them what happens to margins when your design becomes the industry’s design — the victory and the compression are the same event.
  • Watch for the giveaway story. Somebody will say the connector was given away for nothing. Ask them what services revenue grew by, and whether a published standard can be un-published.
  • Watch for the caution story. Somebody will want to cut the 41% research increase to protect a $3.79 billion profit. Ask which architecture we would be absent from for two years.
  • You are allowed to disagree with your own team. The ranking is yours to sign.

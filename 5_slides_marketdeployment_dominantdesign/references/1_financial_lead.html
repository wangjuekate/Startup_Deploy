Case: Tesla, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: which business the capital follows. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the money. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The year, in one paragraph

“In 2025, we recognized total revenues of $94.83 billion, representing a decrease of $2.86 billion compared to the prior year. In 2025, our net income attributable to common stockholders was $3.79 billion, representing a decrease of $3.30 billion compared to the prior year.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

The company produced approximately 1.66 million vehicles and delivered approximately 1.64 million. It deployed 46.7 GWh of energy storage.

The two segments are moving in opposite directions

And so are the margins

Gross margin 2025 2024 2023
Total automotive 17.8% 18.4% 19.4%
Energy generation and storage 29.8% 26.2% 18.9%

Gross profit: automotive $12,361 M; energy $3,802 M; total $17,094 M.

Read the automotive row left to right, then the energy row. One has fallen every year for three years. The other has risen every year for three years, and is now twelve percentage points higher.

What management already did with the money

2025 2024
Research and development $6,411 M (+41%) $4,540 M
R&D as a percentage of revenues 7% 5%
Capital expenditures $8.53 B (−25%) $11.34 B

R&D expense increased “primarily due to increases in costs related to AI and other programs as we continue to expand our product roadmap and technologies.”

Revenue down 3%. Capex down 25%. Research up 41%. Somebody has already voted.

The balance sheet gives you room

Cash and investments $44.06 billion, up $7.50 billion. Cash from operating activities $14.75 billion. “Overall growth has allowed our business to generally fund itself, and we will continue to make critical high-value investments while maintaining a strong balance sheet.”

The headwinds are real

“Certain provisions of the OBBBA, including the removal of tax credits for electric vehicles, may also impact consumer demand for electric vehicles in general.” In 2025 regulatory actions “have restricted certain regulatory credit programs tied to our products” — that revenue fell 28%.

Tariffs “will have a relatively larger impact on our energy generation and storage business compared to our automotive business.”

Your question

Where does the next dollar of capital go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Pull back. Revenue fell $2.86 billion and profit fell $3.30 billion — cut R&D and capex, protect the $3.79 billion, and reinvest when the market recovers.
  • (B) Follow the open gates — energy storage and the AI/autonomy programs. Energy grew 27% at a 29.8% margin while automotive fell 10% at 17.8%; capital belongs where the architecture is still being decided.
  • (C) Fund cost reduction in automotive. It is still $69.5 billion of revenue and 1.64 million deliveries — a point of margin there is worth more than either new business is worth in total.
  • (D) Concentrate on autonomy specifically. R&D is already up 41% to $6.4 billion and Robotaxi is the only thing that changes what a vehicle is worth after the vehicle itself is a commodity.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the gate. Is the thing you are funding at Gate 1 (architecture still open), Gate 2 (the standards battle), or Gate 3 (settled and commoditizing)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Before you decide, answer this out loud: is a falling automotive margin a sign that the company is executing badly, or a sign that it won? Your capital plan depends entirely on which one you think it is.

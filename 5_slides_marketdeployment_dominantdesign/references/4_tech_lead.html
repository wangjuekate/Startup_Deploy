Case: Tesla, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: which architecture the engineering goes to. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the technology bet. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

Where the company says it is going

“We are focused on bringing artificial intelligence (‘AI’) into the real world, through products and services like Full Self-Driving (‘FSD’) (Supervised) and Robotaxi, as well as working to develop and commercialize AI robots (‘Bots’) (including Optimus). We intend to leverage our current operations, in which we design, develop, manufacture, sell and lease high-performance fully electric vehicles and energy generation and storage systems… to achieve that objective.”

— Item 1, Business

The asset that already exists

“We use AI to develop real-world applications… We utilize vision-based technologies, systems and software to train our artificial neural networks, which are accelerated by our own in-house, custom designed inference chips… Every Tesla vehicle delivered today is designed for autonomy, and we believe our capabilities and advancements in AI differentiate us from our competitors.”

Approximately 1.64 million vehicles were delivered in 2025 alone, and every one of them is on the road generating data.

“In June 2025, we launched our Robotaxi service… Our Robotaxi business currently operates with Model Y vehicles but, in time, will include Cybercab, our purpose-built autonomous vehicle.”

The other engineering programme

“The next phase of production growth will be initiated by advances in autonomy and the introduction of new products, including those built on our next generation vehicle platform, as well as our ability to efficiently manufacture our own cells that we are developing to have high-volume output, lower capital and production costs and longer range.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

“We have set ambitious technological targets with our plans for battery cells as well as for iterative manufacturing and design improvements for our vehicles.”

And the third: “We are also capitalizing on our strengths in real-world AI data to advance the development of Optimus, a general purpose, autonomous humanoid robot.”

R&D increased $1.87 billion, or 41%… “primarily due to increases in costs related to AI and other programs as we continue to expand our product roadmap and technologies.”

Revenue fell 3%. Capex fell 25%. Research rose 41%. That is not a rounding error; that is a decision somebody already made.

The competitive picture in the new field

“As we seek to become a top provider of autonomous solutions, we also face competition in the fields of AI and robotics. We expect our Robotaxi service to compete in this developing market, along with traditional ride-hailing and taxi services, through continued progress on our FSD (Supervised) and neural network capabilities, Supercharger network and infotainment offerings.”

— Item 1, Competition

Meanwhile, in the old field: the automotive market “will become even more competitive,” and automotive gross margin has fallen from 19.4% to 18.4% to 17.8%.

Your question

R&D is $6.4 billion and rose 41% in a year revenue fell. Where does the engineering go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Concentrate on autonomy. Every vehicle delivered is already designed for it, so the fleet is the data asset — and the next architecture is being decided in software, not in sheet metal.
  • (B) Spread the budget across autonomy, cells and Optimus. Nobody knows which architecture wins, so keep all three bets alive.
  • (C) Slow the AI spending until Robotaxi proves it can make money. R&D rose 41% to $6.4 billion in a year when revenue fell.
  • (D) Concentrate on in-house cells and the next-generation platform. Cost per vehicle is what keeps us in the market long enough for the software fight to matter.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the gate. Is the architecture you are funding at Gate 1 (still open), Gate 2 (the battle), or Gate 3 (settled)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Sort the three programmes on this page by one test: which of them is a race that has not been decided yet, and which of them is a cost-reduction exercise on a design the whole industry already agrees on? Both are worth doing. Only one of them is a bet.

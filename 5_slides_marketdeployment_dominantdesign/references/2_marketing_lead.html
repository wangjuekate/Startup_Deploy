Case: Tesla, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: how to sell in the first year without the tax credit. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is demand. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The subsidy is gone

“Certain provisions of the OBBBA, including the removal of tax credits for electric vehicles, may also impact consumer demand for electric vehicles in general.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

And on the company’s own side of the ledger:

“In 2025, governmental and regulatory actions, such as OBBBA, have restricted certain regulatory credit programs tied to our products.”

Automotive regulatory credit revenue fell 28%, from $2,763 million to $1,993 million.

What happened to the cars

“Automotive sales revenue decreased $6.66 billion, or 9%… due a decrease of approximately 8% in cash deliveries and a lower average selling price per unit driven by sales mix and higher customer incentives such as attractive financing options.”

2025 2024 2023
Total automotive revenue $69,526 M $77,070 M $82,419 M
Total automotive gross margin 17.8% 18.4% 19.4%

Deliveries: approximately 1.64 million consumer vehicles.

Look at the margin row. It has fallen every year for three years. Price has already been used as a lever — twice.

What grew instead

“Services and other revenue increased $2.00 billion, or 19%… primarily due to increases in paid Supercharging sessions, non-warranty maintenance services and collision revenue, used vehicle sales volume and automotive insurance business revenue.”

Services and other: $12,530 million. Energy generation and storage: $12,771 million, up 27% at a 29.8% gross margin.

The thing a competitor’s car does not come with

“We offer Supercharger access to non-Tesla vehicles in support of our mission, with all major automakers announcing their adoption of the North American Charging Standard (NACS) in certain markets.”

— Item 1, Business

“In June 2025, we launched our Robotaxi service, an autonomous ride-hailing platform… along with products such as FSD (Supervised) subscriptions, unlock the potential to advance a service-driven business model.”

“Every Tesla vehicle delivered today is designed for autonomy.”

Who you are selling against

“The worldwide automotive market is highly competitive and we expect it will become even more competitive in the future as a significant and growing number of established and new automobile manufacturers… have entered, or are reported to have plans to enter the electric vehicle market… Overall, we believe these announcements and vehicle introductions promote the development of the electric vehicle market by highlighting the attractiveness of electric vehicles relative to internal combustion vehicles.”

— Item 1, Competition

Read that last sentence twice. The company is saying that competitors entering the market is good for the market. It is also, unavoidably, what makes an electric car an ordinary purchase rather than a statement.

What management says it is doing about demand

“We will also continue to generate demand by improving our vehicles’ performance and functionality, including through product offerings and features utilizing artificial intelligence such as FSD (Supervised) and other software… In addition, we have been increasing awareness, and expanding our vehicle financing programs, including attractive leasing terms for our customers.”

Your question

The tax credit is gone, deliveries fell 8%, and margin has fallen three years running. How do you sell?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Cut price again to defend volume. 1.64 million deliveries is the number the market watches, and we can make it back on scale.
  • (B) Sell the network and the software rather than the sticker price — paid Supercharging, insurance and services grew 19%, and they are the part a competitor’s car cannot come with.
  • (C) Sell affordability head-on with financing and lease terms rather than price cuts. The credit is gone and monthly payment, not sticker price, is what the buyer actually decides on.
  • (D) Sell the future — market Robotaxi and FSD to make the car the cheapest way into an autonomous fleet.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the gate. Is the thing you are selling at Gate 1 (architecture still open), Gate 2 (the standards battle), or Gate 3 (settled and commoditizing)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

One question before you commit: when every car in the showroom is electric, what is left to sell? Whatever you answer, check whether it is something the buyer can get from the dealership across the street.

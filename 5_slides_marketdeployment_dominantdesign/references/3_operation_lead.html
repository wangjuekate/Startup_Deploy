Case: Tesla, Inc., Form 10-K for fiscal year 2025

You own one decision: what the charging network is for now. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the standard. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

You won

“We have a growing global network of Tesla Superchargers, which are our industrial-grade, high-speed vehicle chargers… Use of the Supercharger network either requires payment of a fee or is free under certain sales programs. We offer Supercharger access to non-Tesla vehicles in support of our mission, with all major automakers announcing their adoption of the North American Charging Standard (NACS) in certain markets. We continue to monitor and increase our network of Tesla Superchargers in anticipation of future demand, including with respect to our Robotaxi services.”

— Item 1, Business

Sit with that sentence. For a decade, the proprietary connector was the reason people said you couldn’t buy this car. Now every major automaker builds to it. The specification is public and adopted. That argument is over.

What the win pays

“Services and other revenue increased $2.00 billion, or 19%… primarily due to increases in paid Supercharging sessions, non-warranty maintenance services and collision revenue, used vehicle sales volume and automotive insurance business revenue.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

Services and other is $12,530 million — larger than the entire energy segment was two years ago, and growing while the cars are not.

The capital-light way to grow it

“To accelerate dependable charging ubiquity, Superchargers are also available through our Supercharger for Business program, where third parties can purchase and install Superchargers and set the charging price, while Tesla remains responsible for software updates, network operations, maintenance and driver support.”

— Item 1

Read who owns what in that sentence. Somebody else buys the steel and picks the price. Tesla keeps the software, the operations, the maintenance and the driver relationship.

In 2025 the company also “opened the first Tesla Diner in California, which offers Supercharging as well as Tesla themed food and merchandise.”

The other customer for charging

“In June 2025, we launched our Robotaxi service, an autonomous ride-hailing platform that harnesses our technology and vehicles.”

And on competing in autonomy:

“We expect our Robotaxi service to compete in this developing market, along with traditional ride-hailing and taxi services, through continued progress on our FSD (Supervised) and neural network capabilities, Supercharger network and infotainment offerings.”

The filing lists the charging network as one of the things Tesla will compete in autonomy with.

Your question

Every major automaker has adopted the connector. What is the network for now?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Aim the build-out at Robotaxi corridors first — the fleet needs dense, reliable charging more than any individual owner does.
  • (B) Scale it through Supercharger for Business — let third parties buy, install and price the hardware while we keep the software, network operations, maintenance and driver support.
  • (C) Keep it open to every automaker and keep operating it ourselves — a standard only pays if everyone uses it, and running the network is what turns other companies’ cars into our revenue.
  • (D) Now that NACS has won, re-prioritise the network for Tesla owners and Robotaxi. We gave the connector away and got nothing back for it.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the gate. Is the network at Gate 1 (architecture still open), Gate 2 (the standards battle), or Gate 3 (settled)?
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Two questions to settle before you pick. Can we un-publish a standard that every major automaker has already adopted? And: when a Ford plugs into our stall, whose customer is that?

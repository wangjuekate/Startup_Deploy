Module 5: Market Deployment and Dominant Design

Start here — Tesla

Read before class (5–8 minutes): the three facts and three terms below. The full blog, company summary, filing, and detailed role pages are optional depth unless a specific passage is assigned.

In class: review the worked example, explain the decision in your own words, then read only your assigned role in the student packet. Four leads make decisions; the Account Manager integrates them. The packet works without SkillSimm access.

Three essential facts

  • The packet reports adoption of Tesla’s connector by major automakers in certain markets.
  • Automotive revenue fell 10%, while energy generation and storage revenue grew 27%.
  • Energy gross margin was 29.8%, compared with 17.8% in automotive.

Three terms

  • Dominant design: an approach widely adopted within a market.
  • Complement: a product or service that makes another offering more useful.
  • Adoption barrier: a reason a customer delays or rejects switching.

Worked example — discuss before opening the simulation

Illustrative startup: an event tool can require a new app or work through a browser already on attendees’ phones. Compare the adoption barrier and the capabilities each approach gives up.

Readiness check: Is your market still choosing a design, or are you building around an established standard? Ask a teammate to explain the task back before starting.

What you produce

  • During the simulation: one decision in words, one supporting figure or passage, one trade-off, and one condition that would change your mind.
  • Account Manager: rank the four lead decisions 1–4 and allocate 100 priority points in blocks of 10; explain the first priority with evidence. These are priority weights, not course-grade points.
  • Before leaving: save your individual decision record. If you finish early, start the venture workshop below.
  • After discussion: submit your individual reflection through the existing HuskyCT assignment. See the reflection guide. Keep the current deadline; from October 20 onward, aim for 300–500 words.

Thursday venture workshop — 15–20 minutes

Choose your venture’s first customer segment. Identify one adoption barrier, one relevant standard or complement, and a low-cost way to test adoption.

Output: A market-entry decision and test for Final Section B. Add it to your shared project document; this is preparation for the project, not a new separately graded assignment.

Debrief question: What did discussion change about your original decision? Explain the evidence, rather than reporting only an option letter.

Takeaway: A widely adopted standard does not automatically establish where profits will come from.

Class rhythm

  • Tuesday (75 minutes): 10 minutes concept and example; 5 minutes task walkthrough; 10 minutes role reading; 25 minutes decisions and team discussion; 15 minutes comparison; 10 minutes saving evidence and starting the venture application.
  • Thursday (75 minutes): 10 minutes concept recap; 35 minutes selected role discussion and feedback; 20 minutes venture workshop; 10 minutes decision record and reflection questions.

Simulation Reflection and Feedback Guide

Start here

  • Who: each student submits an individual reflection.
  • Submit: one 300–500 word reflection, using the five headings below; a .docx or .pdf is accepted. References do not count toward the target.
  • Where: the existing weekly reflection assignment in HuskyCT/Blackboard.
  • When: keep the existing deadline shown in that assignment. The course schedule identifies the reflection day; this update does not change its cutoff time.
  • Use: your saved decision record, the static case packet, and the debrief. You do not need to rejoin a SkillSimm team to write.
  • Length transition: the shorter target applies from the October 20 module onward. Work already submitted under the 500–800 word guidance is not penalized for following it. A complete 500–800 word response remains acceptable.
  • Finished when: all five headings contain a specific answer, evidence is traceable, and the upload opens correctly.

Five response headings

  1. Decision: What did you choose, and what problem were you trying to solve?
  2. Evidence: What figure or specific passage informed the choice? Identify its location and explain what it means.
  3. Trade-off: What did your choice give up? Compare it with one plausible alternative.
  4. Revision: What would you change after discussion, or what evidence would change your mind? Explain why.
  5. Connection: Apply one weekly concept to the decision and explain one implication for your team’s venture.

Reflection rubric — 100 points

Criterion Points What a complete answer does
Decision 20 Identifies a concrete choice and the problem it addresses.
Evidence 20 Uses traceable case evidence and interprets it accurately.
Trade-off 20 Explains an alternative and what is gained or lost.
Revision 20 Explains a change in reasoning or a condition that would change the choice.
Connection 20 Applies a course concept and makes a specific venture connection.

For each criterion: 17–20 = specific and well supported; 13–16 = sound but incompletely explained; 1–12 = partial, generic, or unsupported; 0 = absent. This clarified rubric applies to future reflections, not as a new requirement for work already submitted. Simulation speed, token use, and leaderboard position are not substitutes for the quality of your reasoning.

Feedback you can act on

Each reflection receives a criterion-level score, one specific strength, and one next revision. Where needed, feedback names a factual correction separately.

Illustrative feedback: “Evidence: you compared the two revenue lines using their different margins. Next revision—Trade-off: explain what slower direct-sales growth gives up, and what evidence would justify that sacrifice.”

For project feedback, use the same pattern: section and criterion → evidence in your work → next revision. In the final, record each midterm comment, the change made, and where to find it (or explain a reasoned decision not to change).

Financial Lead

Every loan has to live somewhere. Where should the loans go?

  • A Grow GMV as fast as we can and fund it wherever there is capacity. GMV grew 38% and revenue grew 39% — the funding sorts itself out behind the volume.
  • B Lean toward selling — $15.8 billion sold produced $381.6 million of gain plus $120.6 million of servicing income without tying up our own balance sheet.
  • C Allocate each loan to the channel where its own economics win — hold the ones whose spread beats funding cost and expected losses, sell the rest. The channel is a per-loan decision, not a company-wide policy.
  • D Lean toward holding — interest income is $1.6 billion, our largest single revenue line, and holding the loans is the only way we keep it.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Follow the whole waterfall. Funding decisions affect both income and costs. Explain which lines your choice changes before judging what remains.
  • Compare on a common basis. Annual income, loan balances and loan-sale volume measure different things. State the denominator and time horizon behind your comparison.
  • Stress-test the tradeoff. Explain what happens to contribution and funding capacity if volume doubles or market conditions change. Name an assumption that would change your decision.
📣

Marketing Lead

Marketing spend fell 25% while volume grew 38%, and five partners are now 47% of GMV. Where does the growth money go?

  • A Cut marketing again. It fell 25% and GMV still grew 38% — at this point the network markets itself.
  • B Into the direct-to-consumer products — Affirm Card went from 2% to 10% of transactions, and it makes us the payment method rather than a button on somebody else's checkout page.
  • C Into winning more large enterprise platforms — that is where volume actually lives, and each one adds billions at once.
  • D Into diversifying away from the top five and into repeat usage by consumers we already have — 47% of GMV in five partners is the biggest single risk in the model, and transactions per consumer is the cheapest volume we can add.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Trace growth beyond GMV. Connect your proposed spending to the volume it might generate, the revenue it might earn and the costs that volume would carry.
  • Separate observation from explanation. A lower marketing bill alongside higher volume is a pattern to investigate. Identify what evidence would establish the cause and whether it can continue.
  • Consider who supplies the volume. Customer and partner mix can affect bargaining power and resilience. Explain how those effects enter your waterfall and what your choice gives up.
⚙️

Operation Lead

About 4.75 cents of every GMV dollar leaves as transaction cost, against 8.8 cents of revenue. Which slice do you attack?

  • A Processing and servicing — $457.8 million. Automate collections and customer care so the cost per transaction falls as transactions rise.
  • B Funding costs — $425.5 million. Diversify and extend the facilities so the slice stops moving every time rates do.
  • C Push AOV back up from $273 toward $292 — a bigger basket spreads the same transaction costs over more GMV.
  • D Credit losses — $616.7 million, the largest slice, and the only one we set ourselves at the moment we approve the transaction.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Every subtraction has an owner. Name the process your decision would change, who controls it and how you would measure an improvement.
  • Distinguish dollars from rates. A cost can rise in total while falling per transaction or per GMV dollar. State which measure supports your argument and why.
  • Test the net effect. Changing one slice may also change revenue, service quality or another cost. Trace those effects through contribution before judging the result.
💻

Tech Lead

Technology and data analytics is $589.7 million and the company just posted its first $52.2 million of profit. What do you build and what do you buy?

  • A Hold technology spend flat or cut it. We just turned our first profit of $52.2 million and $589.7 million of engineering is the obvious place to protect it.
  • B Invest hardest in the consumer app and the Affirm Card — going from 2% to 10% of transactions is how we stop depending on somebody else's checkout page.
  • C Buy more from processors and card-issuing partners across the board. We are a network, not an infrastructure company, and engineering is our third-largest expense.
  • D Build the underwriting and risk models — they set the biggest cost in the waterfall and 343 million loans of data cannot be bought — and rent the rails: issuing, processing, core banking.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Connect the budget to the operation. Technology spending can affect several waterfall lines. Explain the path from a proposed investment or cut to an operating outcome.
  • Compare build and buy over time. Consider upfront effort, ongoing fees, control, reliability and dependence on partners. State how those costs behave as usage grows.
  • Make the investment testable. Identify the capability your choice develops, the evidence that would show it working and the tradeoff elsewhere in the business.

Account Manager

  • Allocate 100 priority points in blocks of 10. Explain the first priority with a figure or passage and a dependency.
  • Name a trade-off and evidence that would change the ranking. Save the decision record.

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Trace what each sale keeps after variable costs before recommending growth.
  • Different rankings can be defensible. Connect the priorities to evidence and the weekly concept.
  • Example allocation: 40/30/20/10 totals 100. Explain your own allocation; priority points are not course-grade points.

Key takeaway

Read the business model downward: volume → revenue → transaction costs → contribution → operating expenses. Explain whether your decision improves the economics of each unit, spreads a fixed cost, or changes both. State the assumptions behind that explanation.

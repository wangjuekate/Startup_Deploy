Use after discussion. These takeaways explain the reasoning; compare them with your original decision.

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Follow the whole waterfall. Funding decisions affect both income and costs. Explain which lines your choice changes before judging what remains.
  • Compare on a common basis. Annual income, loan balances and loan-sale volume measure different things. State the denominator and time horizon behind your comparison.
  • Stress-test the tradeoff. Explain what happens to contribution and funding capacity if volume doubles or market conditions change. Name an assumption that would change your decision.

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Trace growth beyond GMV. Connect your proposed spending to the volume it might generate, the revenue it might earn and the costs that volume would carry.
  • Separate observation from explanation. A lower marketing bill alongside higher volume is a pattern to investigate. Identify what evidence would establish the cause and whether it can continue.
  • Consider who supplies the volume. Customer and partner mix can affect bargaining power and resilience. Explain how those effects enter your waterfall and what your choice gives up.

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Every subtraction has an owner. Name the process your decision would change, who controls it and how you would measure an improvement.
  • Distinguish dollars from rates. A cost can rise in total while falling per transaction or per GMV dollar. State which measure supports your argument and why.
  • Test the net effect. Changing one slice may also change revenue, service quality or another cost. Trace those effects through contribution before judging the result.

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Connect the budget to the operation. Technology spending can affect several waterfall lines. Explain the path from a proposed investment or cut to an operating outcome.
  • Compare build and buy over time. Consider upfront effort, ongoing fees, control, reliability and dependence on partners. State how those costs behave as usage grows.
  • Make the investment testable. Identify the capability your choice develops, the evidence that would show it working and the tradeoff elsewhere in the business.

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Trace what each sale keeps after variable costs before recommending growth.
  • Different rankings can be defensible. Connect the priorities to evidence and the weekly concept.
  • Example allocation: 40/30/20/10 totals 100. Explain your own allocation; priority points are not course-grade points.

Key takeaway

  • Read the business model downward: volume → revenue → transaction costs → contribution → operating expenses. Explain whether your decision improves the economics of each unit, spreads a fixed cost, or changes both. State the assumptions behind that explanation.
  • At double the volume, what changes in your waterfall — and what evidence would make you reconsider?

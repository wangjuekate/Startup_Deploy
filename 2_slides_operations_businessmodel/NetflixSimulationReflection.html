Affirm at the Waterfall

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, September 17

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • As a group, name the slice of the waterfall your decision touches and whether that slice is variable or fixed.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and defense.
💰

Financial Lead

Held on balance sheet, a loan produces interest income ($1,608.2 M — the largest revenue line) plus funding cost ($425.5 M) and credit provision ($616.7 M). Sold, it produces gain on sale ($381.6 M on $15.8 B sold) plus servicing income ($120.6 M). Where should the loans go?

  • A Per-loan: hold where the spread beats funding cost and expected losses; sell the rest.
  • B Lean toward selling — capital-light, $381.6 M of gain plus $120.6 M of servicing.
  • C Lean toward holding — interest income is the largest revenue line and holding is how we keep it.
  • D Grow GMV as fast as we can and fund it wherever there's capacity.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Treat the funding channel as an economics question, not a policy: $15.8 B sold produced $381.6 M of gain; holding keeps the interest spread but carries $425.5 M of funding cost and $616.7 M of provision.
  • Reasoning from a gross revenue line (interest income) rather than a net one is how a lender books record growth and a record loss in the same year.
  • The company's own words: it "proactively manages the allocation of loans… across various funding channels."
📣

Marketing Lead

Sales and marketing fell 25% ($576.4 M → $434.8 M) while GMV grew 38%. The top five partners are ~47% of GMV, with Amazon alone 22%. Transactions per consumer went 4.9 → 5.8; the Affirm Card went 2% → 10% of transactions. Where does growth money go?

  • A Diversify away from the top five; grow repeat usage among consumers we already have.
  • B Into the direct-to-consumer products — the Affirm Card and the App.
  • C Into winning more large enterprise platforms — that's where volume lives.
  • D Cut marketing again — it fell 25% and GMV still grew 38%.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Marketing fell 25% because growth came from five partners — now 47% of GMV, up from 42% and rising. Reading that as "the network markets itself" mistakes a concentration risk for an efficiency gain.
  • The cheapest volume available is consumers already on the platform, whose transaction frequency went 4.9 → 5.8.
  • The Affirm Card going 2% → 10% of transactions is how the company stops being a button on someone else's checkout page.
⚙️

Operation Lead

About 4.75 cents of every GMV dollar leaves as transaction cost: credit $616.7 M, processing $457.8 M, funding $425.5 M, purchase commitment $242.3 M — all four grow with volume. AOV fell $292 → $273 as repeat usage rose. Which slice do you attack?

  • A Credit losses — the largest slice, and the one we set ourselves at approval.
  • B Funding costs — diversify and extend the facilities.
  • C Processing and servicing — automate collections and customer care.
  • D Push AOV back up toward $292 to spread transaction costs over more GMV.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Credit losses are the largest slice ($616.7 M) and the one the company sets itself at the moment of approval — operating skill converts directly into margin.
  • AOV fell deliberately: $292 → $273 came with transactions per consumer rising 4.9 → 5.8. "Push AOV back up" reverses the strategy, not the cost.
  • All four transaction-cost slices scale with volume — growth multiplies them; it does not dilute them.
💻

Tech Lead

Technology and data analytics is $589.7 M (+18%) — underwriting models built on 343 million loans, transaction-level risk pricing, the App and the Card, plus plumbing partners already provide. The first profit was $52.2 M. What do you build and what do you buy?

  • A Build underwriting and risk models; rent the rails (issuing, processing, core banking).
  • B Invest hardest in the consumer app and the Affirm Card.
  • C Buy more from processors and issuing partners across the board.
  • D Hold technology spend flat or cut it to protect the $52.2 M profit.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The underwriting model is the business model: it sets the largest variable slice of the waterfall and is built from 343M loans nobody else has.
  • Issuing and processing are rentable — every competitor rents them. Outsourcing the risk models would outsource the only advantage the filing actually claims.
  • Cutting the function that sets a $616.7 M cost line to protect $52.2 M trades the largest variable slice for a rounding error.
🔒

Security Lead

Affirm approves strangers in milliseconds, holds data behind 343 million loans, depends on bank partners for rate authority, and runs a NIST-framework program that "cannot provide assurance" about the future. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Underwriting integrity, fraud and data protection as one system; same bar for third parties.
  • B Concentrate on the third-party surface — bank partners, processors, loan buyers, servicers.
  • C Concentrate on regulatory compliance — the rate authority is the product's licence to exist.
  • D Loosen approvals slightly to keep GMV growing; the 5.6% allowance already covers it.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • In a lending business, underwriting, fraud and data are the same system: the models that price risk are the most valuable thing to manipulate, and the consumer data behind them the most valuable thing to steal.
  • An allowance is an estimate of losses already expected — not headroom to create new ones. Loosening approvals raises the largest slice to buy volume worth less per dollar.
  • Compliance protects the licence to operate; it does not protect the operation.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question.

After all five leads present their selection + one-sentence defense, rank the five decisions 1–5 by which the company must resolve first, allocate 100 points across them in blocks of 10, and name the variable slices: which parts of this waterfall get thicker when volume doubles, and which get thinner?

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Rank by what happens to each dollar on the way down — the waterfall and funding belong above concentration and tech, and the rank-#1 sentence must contain a figure.
  • A flat 20/20/20/20/20 says everything matters equally, which means the Account Manager decided nothing.
  • $52.2 M of profit on $36.7 B of volume is one-seventh of one cent per dollar — and the only number on the page that can go back to zero.

Key takeaway

Read a business model downward: volume → take → variable subtractions → contribution → fixed stack. The business model is whatever survives the waterfall — and growth multiplies the leaks unless you fix them first.

GMV is not revenue. Revenue is not contribution.

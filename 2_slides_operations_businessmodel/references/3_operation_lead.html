Operation Lead

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Affirm Holdings, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025

You own one decision: which slice of the waterfall to attack. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the cost of every dollar of volume. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The top of the waterfall

GMV was $36.7 billion in fiscal 2025, up 38%. Total revenue, net was $3,224.4 million — about 8.8 cents of every GMV dollar.

The four subtractions that sit directly under revenue

Transaction cost, FY2025 $ vs FY2024 ≈ % of GMV
Provision for credit losses $616.7 M +34% 1.68%
Processing and servicing $457.8 M +33% 1.25%
Funding costs $425.5 M +24% 1.16%
Loss on loan purchase commitment $242.3 M +34% 0.66%
Total $1,742.1 M 4.75%

Every one of these grows when volume grows. Here is what the filing says drives each:

Provision for credit losses — “management’s estimate of future losses… determined based on our estimate of expected future losses on loans originated during the period.” The allowance was 5.6% of loans held for investment, up from 5.5%; average loans held for investment was $6.5 billion, up 28%.

Processing and servicing — “payment processing fees, third-party customer support and collection expense, salaries and personnel-related costs of our customer care team, platform fees.” Payment processing fees rose $69.5 million, or 36%, on a 36% rise in payment volume; customer service and collection fees rose 47%.

Funding costs — “interest expense and the amortization of fees for certain borrowings collateralized by our loans.” Average funding debt $5.9 billion, up 30%.

Loss on loan purchase commitment — incurred “on a per loan basis” when the company must buy a loan from a bank partner “for a price in excess of the fair market value.” The company purchased $30.0 billion of loans (+40%), of which $4.4 billion were 0% APR (+65%).

What the company says its edge is

“We believe that our technology, underwriting, and risk management are key competitive advantages. Our proprietary technology’s ability to price and assess risk at a transaction level provides a unique advantage compared to legacy payment and credit systems. Our approach to risk management is core to our business model and has led to lower fraud rates, higher approval rates compared to traditional credit underwriting models, and lower credit losses.”

— Item 1, Business

The models are “built on extensive data points, including data from approximately 343 million loans.”

The basket is getting smaller — on purpose

“The decrease in AOV is driven by the diversification of our merchant base, with accelerated growth in some of our largest interest-bearing merchant programs, and our ongoing initiative to drive repeat usage of our platform beyond one-time high AOV purchases.”

— Item 7

What is left at the bottom

Operating loss $(87.3) million. Net income $52.2 million — the first profitable year, on $36.7 billion of volume.

Your question

About 4.75 cents of every GMV dollar leaves as transaction cost, against 8.8 cents of revenue. Which slice do you attack?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Processing and servicing — $457.8 million. Automate collections and customer care so the cost per transaction falls as transactions rise.
  • (B) Funding costs — $425.5 million. Diversify and extend the facilities so the slice stops moving every time rates do.
  • (C) Push AOV back up from $273 toward $292 — a bigger basket spreads the same transaction costs over more GMV.
  • (D) Credit losses — $616.7 million, the largest slice, and the only one we set ourselves at the moment we approve the transaction.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the slice of the waterfall your choice touches — and say whether it is variable (grows with volume) or fixed (diluted by volume).
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Two tests before you commit. Which slice is biggest? And which slice do we actually set ourselves, rather than being quoted it by a bank, a card network, or the bond market? Your best answer is probably the slice that scores well on both.

Case: Affirm Holdings, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025

You own one decision: what gets built in-house and what gets rented. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the software. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

What the technology actually does here

“We believe that our technology, underwriting, and risk management are key competitive advantages. Our proprietary technology’s ability to price and assess risk at a transaction level provides a unique advantage compared to legacy payment and credit systems. Our approach to risk management is core to our business model and has led to lower fraud rates, higher approval rates compared to traditional credit underwriting models, and lower credit losses.”

— Item 1, Business

“Our models have been built on extensive data points, including data from approximately 343 million loans. Furthermore, our risk management models are designed to continuously improve over time, becoming more precise and efficient with each transaction.”

Read that next to the cost table below and notice what it means: the underwriting model is not a feature. It is the thing that sets the single largest cost in the business.

Note also that $178.5 million of stock-based compensation was capitalized into property, equipment and software in fiscal 2025 (up from $126.5 M) — a large part of the engineering effort is being built into assets, not expensed.

What is built on top of it

  • AdaptAI — described as “Affirm’s AI-powered personalized promotion platform.”
  • The Affirm Card — “a debit card that can be used physically or virtually and which allows consumers to link a bank account to pay in full, or apply to pay over time.” It went from ~2% of transactions to ~10% in two years.
  • One-time-use virtual cards, “allowing them to shop with merchants that are not integrated with Affirm.”
  • Affirm at Checkout — “our direct Application Programming Interface (API)… allows merchants to easily incorporate Affirm into their payment and product pages with minimal investment.”

What is rented

The company depends on originating bank partners (Cross River Bank, Celtic Bank) to originate loans and to establish interest rates, on card-issuing partners to issue the Affirm Card, and on issuer processors — “$11.9 billion of GMV processed through our issuer processors, an increase of 45%.”

The money situation

Operating loss $(87.3) million. Net income $52.2 million — the first profitable year in the company’s history, on $36.7 billion of GMV.

Who else is out there

“Many of our competitors are substantially larger than we are… [with] more versatile technology platforms… and lower-cost funding.” Competitors named include Visa, MasterCard, PayPal, Block and Klarna.

Your question

Technology and data analytics is $589.7 million and the company just posted its first $52.2 million of profit. What do you build and what do you buy?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Hold technology spend flat or cut it. We just turned our first profit of $52.2 million and $589.7 million of engineering is the obvious place to protect it.
  • (B) Invest hardest in the consumer app and the Affirm Card — going from 2% to 10% of transactions is how we stop depending on somebody else’s checkout page.
  • (C) Buy more from processors and card-issuing partners across the board. We are a network, not an infrastructure company, and engineering is our third-largest expense.
  • (D) Build the underwriting and risk models — they set the biggest cost in the waterfall and 343 million loans of data cannot be bought — and rent the rails: issuing, processing, core banking.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the slice of the waterfall your choice touches — and say whether it is variable (grows with volume) or fixed (diluted by volume).
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Sort every system on this page into two piles: the ones a competitor could rent tomorrow and the ones they could not buy at any price. Then ask which pile is connected to the $616.7 million line.

Account Manager — Company Overview

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Affirm Holdings, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025

You answer no functional question of your own. The other four leads each own one decision and one page. You own the whole company — and the order in which its problems get solved. Your page is the overview. Read it before they present.

The company, in the filing’s own terms

“Affirm was founded in 2012 with a mission to deliver honest financial products that improve lives. We are building the next generation payment network.”

— Item 1, Business

A consumer buys something. Affirm pays the merchant now and lets the consumer pay over time. The company earns a fee from the merchant for converting the sale, and interest from the consumer on the loans that carry interest. Since founding it has charged “$0 in late fees for missed payments. We do not profit from consumers’ mistakes.”

The loans themselves are originated by partner banks, purchased by Affirm, and then either held on its balance sheet or sold to third-party buyers and securitizations — with Affirm continuing to service them either way.

Three products: Pay-in-X (short-term, 0% APR), 0% APR monthly installment loans, and interest-bearing monthly installment loans.

Part 3 — Name the variable slices

Out loud, to the team: which slices of this waterfall get thicker when volume doubles, and which get thinner? A fixed cost is a problem you can outgrow. A variable cost is one you must out-operate.

How to run the room

  • Rank by what happens to each dollar on the way down, not by volume of voice.
  • You may not rank #1 anything a lead could not defend with a number.
  • Watch for the growth story. Somebody will tell you GMV grew 38% and revenue grew 39%, so the model is compounding. Ask what each of those dollars kept. The operating line was still negative $87.3 million; the profit came from $148.7 million of other income.
  • Watch for the efficiency story. Somebody will tell you marketing fell 25% while volume grew 38%. Ask whose volume that was — concentration went from 42% to 47%.
  • Watch for “the allowance covers it.” An allowance measures losses already expected. It is not permission to create more.
  • You are allowed to disagree with your own team. The ranking is yours to sign.

Marketing Lead

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Affirm Holdings, Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2025

You own one decision: where the growth money goes. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is who the volume comes from. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The strange thing about last year

FY2025 FY2024 Change
GMV $36.7 B $26.6 B +38%
Sales and marketing expense $434.8 M $576.4 M −25%

Volume grew 38% while the marketing budget shrank by a quarter. Somebody in the room is going to read that as proof the network now markets itself. Before you agree, read the next section.

Where the volume actually came from

“The top five merchants and platform partners as of June 30, 2025 and 2024 represented approximately 47% of total GMV. The top five merchants and platform partners as of June 30, 2023 represented approximately 42% of total GMV. For the years ended June 30, 2025, 2024, and 2023, GMV attributable to Amazon represented 22%, 21% and less than 20%, respectively, of total GMV.”

— Item 7, Key Operating Metrics

Concentration did not hold steady. It went up — 42% to 47% in two years. The company has approximately 377 thousand active merchants, and five of them are nearly half the business.

The other engine — the consumers you already have

FY2025 FY2024 FY2023
Active consumers (millions) 23.0 18.7 16.5
Transactions per active consumer 5.8 4.9 3.9
AOV $273 $292 —

“The increase was primarily due to a high retention rate of existing consumers, as well as continued adoption of the Affirm Card, and the acquisition of new consumers through an expansion in active merchants and platform partnerships.”

— Item 7

“As of June 30, 2025, Affirm Card represented approximately 10% of the total number of transactions compared to approximately 8% and 2% as of June 30, 2024 and 2023.”

And the AOV decline is deliberate:

“The decrease in AOV is driven by the diversification of our merchant base… and our ongoing initiative to drive repeat usage of our platform beyond one-time high AOV purchases.”

Who you are competing with

“Our primary competition consists of: legacy payment methods, such as credit and debit cards… technology solutions provided by payment companies such as Visa and MasterCard; mobile wallets such as PayPal; and other pay-over-time solutions offered by companies such as Block and Klarna… Additionally, some merchants are increasingly offering proprietary pay-over-time options to customers, and in some cases, these are presented parallel to our offerings at checkout.”

— Item 1, Competition

“Many of our competitors are substantially larger than we are… [with] the ability to cross-subsidize their offerings through their other business lines… and lower-cost funding.”

What the company promises consumers

Since founding, “$0 in late fees for missed payments. We do not profit from consumers’ mistakes.”

Your question

Marketing spend fell 25% while volume grew 38%, and five partners are now 47% of GMV. Where does the growth money go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Cut marketing again. It fell 25% and GMV still grew 38% — at this point the network markets itself.
  • (B) Into the direct-to-consumer products — Affirm Card went from 2% to 10% of transactions, and it makes us the payment method rather than a button on somebody else’s checkout page.
  • (C) Into winning more large enterprise platforms — that is where volume actually lives, and each one adds billions at once.
  • (D) Into diversifying away from the top five and into repeat usage by consumers we already have — 47% of GMV in five partners is the biggest single risk in the model, and transactions per consumer is the cheapest volume we can add.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the slice of the waterfall your choice touches — and say whether it is variable (grows with volume) or fixed (diluted by volume).
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Here is the question underneath your question: when a partner who is 22% of your volume sits down to renegotiate, what do you say? Whatever your answer to that is, it has to be paid for a year in advance, out of the budget you are deciding about right now.

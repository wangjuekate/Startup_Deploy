Account Manager — Company Overview

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Snowflake Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You answer no functional question of your own. The other four leads each own one decision and one page. You own the whole company — and the order in which its problems get solved. Your page is the overview. Read it before they present.

The company, in the filing’s own terms

“We deliver the AI Data Cloud, a network where Snowflake customers, partners, developers, data providers, and data consumers can break down data silos and derive value from a growing number of data sets… We provide our platform through a customer-centric, consumption-based business model.”

— Item 1, Business

Customers put their data on the platform and run work against it. The architecture is “three independently scalable but logically integrated layers across storage, compute, and cloud services,” running on “three major public clouds across 53 regional deployments.”

And this is the whole case: the invoice is not a subscription and not a seat count. It is

“fees charged to our customers based on the compute, storage, and data transfer resources consumed on our platform… customers pay only for what they use.”

Contracts are capacity arrangements of one to four years, or on-demand monthly. But —

“Because we generally recognize product revenue on consumption and not ratably… we do not have visibility into the timing of revenue recognition from any particular customer… our deferred revenue is not a meaningful indicator of future revenue.”

13,328 total customers (from 10,996). The 790 Global 2000 customers are ~43% of revenue. $3.1 billion of unvested equity awards remain to be recognised over 2.7 years. G&A includes $108.7 M of impairment from the cease-use of the San Mateo office.

Three facts to hold at once: 1. A $1.4 billion operating loss and $1.1 billion of positive free cash flow — the gap is mostly the $1.6 billion of stock compensation. 2. Existing customers still expand (125%), but the rate has fallen 133 → 126 → 125, and under consumption pricing that needs no cancellation. 3. The filing states its own improvements “may make our platform more efficient, enabling customers to consume fewer… resources to accomplish the same workloads… we may experience lower revenue.” Open formats let customers use compute “without requiring storage.”

The roadmap: Snowpark, Container Services, Snowflake Intelligence, Cortex Agents, Generation 2 Warehouses, Snowflake Postgres (a “new transactions product category”). Acquired Crunchy Data ($164.5 M) and Observe (~$596.2 M, AI-powered observability).

The competition: “AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform” — which “generally compete in all of our markets” — plus legacy database vendors, observability vendors, new entrants. Named competitive factors include “pricing transparency and optimized price-performance” and “interoperability… across open formats.”

Security: a shared-responsibility model; the company “do[es] not have visibility into whether adequate access controls (such as MFA or network restrictions) are being enforced” by customers using third-party auth, and customer failures “may harm our… reputation… which has occurred in the past.”

  3. Name one fence you would add — a way for the customers who get more value to pay more, without losing the ones who get less.

How to run the room

  • Rank by what happens to the meter, not by volume of voice.
  • You may not rank #1 anything a lead could not defend with a number.
  • Watch for the commitment story. Somebody will point at $9,771.5 million of remaining performance obligations and call it secured revenue. The filing says revenue is recognised on consumption, and that deferred revenue “is not a meaningful indicator of future revenue.”
  • Watch for the cost-cutting story. Somebody will want to cut the $1,969.5 million of R&D. Ask what creates the next workload — and what the $1,599.5 million of stock compensation has to do with the loss.
  • Watch for the efficiency story. Somebody will suggest shipping less efficiency to bill more. Ask who else is selling this customer compute. The answer is AWS, Azure and Google.
  • Watch for the contract story. Somebody will want to fix trust with clearer terms. Ask whose name is in the headline.
  • You are allowed to disagree with your own team. The ranking is yours to sign.

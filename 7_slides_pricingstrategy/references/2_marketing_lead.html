Marketing Lead

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Snowflake Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You own one decision: where the selling effort goes. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is how accounts grow. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

How an account grows here

“Once our platform has been adopted, we focus on increasing the migration of additional customer workloads to our platform to drive increased consumption, as evidenced by our net revenue retention rate of 125% and 126% as of January 31, 2026 and 2025.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

Notice the mechanism. The account does not grow because somebody negotiated a bigger number. It grows because another workload moved onto the platform and started consuming.

The number that should hold your attention

As of January 31, 2026 2025 2024
Net revenue retention rate 125% 126% 133%
Customers with trailing 12-month product revenue > $1 million 733 576 449
Forbes Global 2000 customers 790 750 716
Remaining performance obligations (in millions) $9,771.5 $6,867.5 $5,174.7

Two true things at once. Existing customers still spend 125 cents for every dollar they spent last year — that is expansion, and most companies would take it. And the rate has fallen eight points in two years.

Here is the part that matters for your job: under consumption pricing, that decline needs no cancellation. Nobody has to churn. Nobody has to call. The meter simply runs slower.

The base

“As of January 31, 2026, we had 13,328 total customers, increasing from 10,996 customers as of January 31, 2025… our customers included 790 of the Forbes Global 2000… and those customers contributed approximately 43% of our revenue.”

Your budget

FY2026 FY2025 FY2024
Sales and marketing $2,062.1 M (44% of revenue) $1,672.1 M (46%) $1,391.7 M (50%)
Revenue $4,683.9 M $3,626.4 M $2,806.5 M
Product revenue $4,472.3 M $3,462.4 M $2,666.8 M

The company sells “primarily through a direct sales force, which targets technical and business leaders,” plus resellers and distributors.

What the filing warns about commitments

“Because we generally recognize product revenue on consumption and not ratably over the term of the contract, we do not have visibility into the timing of revenue recognition from any particular customer… For these reasons, we believe our deferred revenue is not a meaningful indicator of future revenue that will be recognized in any given time period.”

Somebody in the room is going to point at $9,771.5 million of remaining performance obligations and call it secured revenue. Read that quote again before you agree.

What customers are doing

“Many customers are attempting to rationalize budgets, prioritize cash flow management, and optimize consumption amidst macroeconomic uncertainty. In any given period, there is a risk that customer consumption of our platform will be slower than we expect.”

Adverse conditions have also caused “shortened contract duration” and “reducing storage through shorter data retention policies.”

Who you are up against

“Large, well-established, public cloud providers that generally compete in all of our markets, including Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure (Azure), and Google Cloud Platform (GCP)”* — plus legacy database vendors, observability vendors and new entrants.

Your question

Net revenue retention has fallen from 133% to 125%. Where does the selling effort go?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Into locking in longer capacity commitments. Remaining performance obligations rose to $9,771.5 million — the more customers commit up front, the less any of this matters.
  • (B) Into new logos. Total customers went from 10,996 to 13,328, and a wider base is what stops any single expansion rate from deciding the year.
  • (C) Into migrating new workloads inside existing customers. Under a consumption metric, revenue grows when work moves onto the platform — and the $1 million-plus customer count going from 449 to 733 is what that looks like when it works.
  • (D) Into the largest accounts. 790 Forbes Global 2000 customers are already about 43% of revenue and they have the most workloads left to move.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the corner of the triangle your choice moves — the metric (what we charge for), the level (how much), or the fences (who pays more).
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

One question to settle first: what, exactly, makes the meter run faster next quarter? If your answer is “a signature,” check the filing’s sentence about deferred revenue. If your answer is “another workload,” you have found the job.

Operation Lead

Detailed reference, not required in full during the timed activity. Start with the short student packet; use this page to investigate or cite supporting evidence.

Case: Snowflake Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You own one decision: what to do about the efficiency paradox. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the one where getting better costs money. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

The meter you are running

“We generate the substantial majority of our revenue from fees charged to our customers based on the compute, storage, and data transfer resources consumed on our platform. Customers are allowed to select compute, storage, and data transfer resources separately, at their discretion. For compute resources, consumption is based on the type of compute resource used and the duration of use or, for some features, the volume of data processed. For storage resources, consumption… is based on the average terabytes per month… For data transfer resources, consumption is based on terabytes of data transferred.”

— Item 7, Management’s Discussion and Analysis

Three meters. Every one of them measures resources burned, not value delivered.

The sentence your entire page is about

“New software releases or hardware improvements, like better storage compression, cloud infrastructure processor improvements, and compute optimization, may make our platform more efficient, enabling customers to consume fewer compute, storage, and data transfer resources to accomplish the same workloads. In addition, open data formats allow customers to use our platform for compute services without requiring storage. To the extent these improvements do not result in an offsetting increase in new workloads, we may experience lower revenue.”

Read the last clause carefully. It is a conditional, not a prediction. The revenue falls to the extent the freed budget does not come back as new work.

And the customers are actively trying

“Many customers are attempting to rationalize budgets, prioritize cash flow management, and optimize consumption amidst macroeconomic uncertainty.”

Adverse conditions have caused “shortened contract duration, and optimize consumption, including by reducing storage through shorter data retention policies.”

What efficiency is doing to the cost side

Gross margin has been essentially flat. Efficiency gains have not been going into margin — they have been going to the customer, as a lower bill.

Why you cannot simply stop

The company lists its own competitive factors, and two of them are:

“ability to provide pricing transparency and optimized price-performance benefits”

“ability to elastically scale up and scale down in high-intensity use cases”

“ability to provide interoperability and integrations across open formats, tools, applications, and platforms”

Against these competitors:

“Amazon Web Services (AWS), Microsoft Azure (Azure), and Google Cloud Platform (GCP)”* — who “generally compete in all of our markets.”

And the platform now spans more kinds of work than it used to: Snowpark, Snowpark Container Services, Snowflake Intelligence and Cortex Agents, Generation 2 Standard Warehouses, and Snowflake Postgres in a “new transactions product category.”

Your question

Every efficiency improvement you ship lets customers pay you less for the same work. What do you do about it?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Slow down the efficiency work on existing workloads and direct engineering to features we can charge separately for. There is no reason to cut our own meter.
  • (B) Ship the efficiency and go get the freed budget back as new workloads — the filing itself says lower revenue follows only “to the extent these improvements do not result in an offsetting increase in new workloads.”
  • (C) Help customers optimize deliberately and openly. Price-performance is a factor we say we compete on, and the customer who trusts our optimization advice keeps the next workload with us.
  • (D) Broaden what can be metered. Transactions, AI agents, observability and applications put more kinds of work on the same meter, so one workload getting cheaper matters less.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the corner of the triangle your choice moves — the metric (what we charge for), the level (how much), or the fences (who pays more).
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

One question decides your page: when a customer’s bill goes down because we made the product better, where does that money go? If the honest answer is “to their other vendors,” you have found your job. If it is “nowhere, they just keep it,” you have found a different job.

Case: Snowflake Inc., Form 10-K for the fiscal year ended January 31, 2026

You own one decision: what engineering builds. Everyone on your team has a different page. Yours is the roadmap. Read it, pick one option, and be ready to defend it with a number.

What the filing says

Your budget

FY2026 FY2025 FY2024
Research and development $1,969,472 K (42% of revenue) $1,783,379 K (49%) $1,287,949 K (46%)
Revenue $4,683,946 K $3,626,396 K $2,806,489 K

Of that, $935.4 million was stock-based compensation — R&D alone is 20% of revenue in equity.

What has already been built

“In 2021, we launched Snowpark for Java and Scala… in 2022 we added support for Python… In 2023, we launched Snowpark Container Services, a fully managed container platform… In 2024 and 2025, we announced and then launched Snowflake Intelligence within our AI product category, to enable our customers to create data agents… In 2025 and 2026, we announced and then launched Snowflake Postgres within our new transactions product category, a fully managed Postgres offering that enables customers to consolidate application and analytical data onto a single platform.”

— Item 1, Business

Also launched in fiscal 2026: “Snowflake Cortex Agents, Generation 2 Standard Warehouses” and more.

And bought:

On June 6, 2025, the company acquired Crunchy Data — “a privately-held company that provided PostgreSQL technology” — for $164.5 million in cash.

On February 2, 2026, it acquired Observe, Inc. — “a privately-held company that built an AI-powered observability platform” — for approximately $596.2 million.

Two acquisitions, two new categories of work that can run on the platform.

The architecture you are building on

“Our cloud-native architecture includes three independently scalable but logically integrated layers across storage, compute, and cloud services… This architecture is built on three major public clouds across 53 regional deployments around the world.”

And here is what makes your job strange

The company charges for consumption — “the compute, storage, and data transfer resources consumed.” Which produces this warning, in the company’s own words:

“New software releases or hardware improvements, like better storage compression, cloud infrastructure processor improvements, and compute optimization, may make our platform more efficient, enabling customers to consume fewer compute, storage, and data transfer resources to accomplish the same workloads. In addition, open data formats allow customers to use our platform for compute services without requiring storage. To the extent these improvements do not result in an offsetting increase in new workloads, we may experience lower revenue.”

Read the middle sentence twice. Open formats do not just reduce the storage bill — they can remove one of the three things being metered from the invoice entirely.

What the company says it needs

“Our future growth is increasingly dependent on our ability to increase consumption of our platform by building and expanding the AI Data Cloud.”

“We will continue to invest significantly in research and development to improve our platform, including in the areas of data science and AI Technology.”

The competitive factors it names

“Ability to support multiple use cases in one platform”; “interoperability and integrations across open formats, tools, applications, and platforms”; “use of AI and timely availability of leading, enterprise-grade large language models and AI agents”; and “pricing transparency and optimized price-performance benefits.”

Against AWS, Azure and GCP, who “generally compete in all of our markets.”

Your question

R&D is $1.97 billion — 42% of revenue — and the revenue model is paid by the unit consumed. What does engineering build?

Your four choices — pick one

  • (A) Build price-performance improvements on the core warehouse. It is the biggest part of consumption and the competitive factor we name first.
  • (B) Build the AI layer hardest. Snowflake Intelligence and Cortex Agents are where new workloads are actually being created right now, and new workloads are what the revenue model needs.
  • (C) Build for open formats and interoperability. Customers are going there anyway, and being the best compute engine over open data keeps us in the transaction even when we lose the storage.
  • (D) Build the new categories that put more kinds of work on the meter — transactions, AI agents, observability — because a consumption model is only as big as the range of work it can measure.

Before you answer

A competent lead does four things, in this order:

  1. Name the corner of the triangle your choice moves — the metric (what we charge for), the level (how much), or the fences (who pays more).
  2. Cite at least one figure from this page. Not a feeling — a number.
  3. Name what your choice gives up. Every option costs you something.
  4. State one thing that would change your answer. If nothing would, you are not reasoning.

Sort every item on the roadmap by one test: does this create work that has never run here before, or does it make work that already runs here cheaper? Both are worth building. Only one of them makes the invoice bigger.

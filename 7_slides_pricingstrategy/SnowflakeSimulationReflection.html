  • B.35 units: recapture (40) and migration (25) use 65 of the 100-unit ceiling.
  • C.0 units: the recapture program alone uses the whole ceiling.
  • D.60 units: the migration motion is a sales cost and sits outside the ceiling.

Stage 1 — Key takeaways

Understand what the meter rewards

Pricing Strategy: Connect the value metric to customer behavior.

  • Combine the private evidence. The CFO holds the ceiling and dilution rule; the COO prices recapture; the CMO prices migration; the CTO explains the engineering choices.
  • Calculate the real cost. Recapture (40) and migration (25) leave 35 of the 100 planning units. The AI layer costs 30 × 1.5 = 45 because it uses new equity awards.
  • Trace the dependency. Efficiency lowers consumption for the same work. Recapture needs the migration motion, and new metered categories give customers more work to move.

Simulation assumptions: planning units, the ceiling, the equity multiplier and option dependencies.

Stage 2 — Make Executive Decisions

Chief Financial Officer (CFO)

Revenue grew 29% to $4,683.9 million at a 67% gross margin, but the operating loss was $(1,435.2) million while free cash flow was a positive $1,120.3 million. Stock-based compensation was $1,599.5 million — 34% of revenue — with $3.1 billion of unvested awards still to come. How should Snowflake fund this round?

  • A.Cut research and development; at $1,969.5 million it is the largest expense line, and closing the loss comes first.
  • B.Do nothing structural; free cash flow is positive, so fund the round from the $1,120.3 million it generated on top of the plan.
  • C.Fund a coordinated recapture plan across operations, go-to-market and engineering within the ceiling, paid in cash rather than new equity awards, so stock-based compensation keeps falling as a share of revenue.
  • D.Fund the AI layer first with new equity grants, since awards use no cash, and fund operations and go-to-market from what remains.

Stage 2 — Make Executive Decisions

Chief Marketing Officer (CMO)

Net revenue retention fell from 133% to 126% to 125%. Customers grew from 10,996 to 13,328, $1 million-plus customers from 449 to 733, and the 790 Forbes Global 2000 customers are about 43% of revenue. Remaining performance obligations rose to $9,771.5 million. Where does the go-to-market effort go?

  • A.Hunt new logos; a wider base stops any single expansion rate from deciding the year.
  • B.Lock in longer capacity commitments; remaining performance obligations of $9,771.5 million show customers will commit up front.
  • C.Concentrate on the largest accounts; the Forbes Global 2000 have the most workloads left to move.
  • D.Migrate new workloads inside existing customers, timed to the COO’s recapture program, so freed budget returns to the meter.

Stage 2 — Make Executive Decisions

Chief Operating Officer (COO)

The filing warns that improvements may let customers “consume fewer compute, storage, and data transfer resources to accomplish the same workloads” and that without “an offsetting increase in new workloads, we may experience lower revenue.” Customers are already optimizing consumption. What does operations do with the next efficiency release?

  • A.Ship the efficiency release on its own; price-performance is the factor we compete on first.
  • B.Ship the efficiency release with the workload-recapture program, paired with the CMO’s migration motion, so freed budget comes back as new workloads.
  • C.Slow the efficiency work on existing workloads and steer engineering toward separately chargeable features; there is no reason to cut our own meter.
  • D.Run an open optimization program that helps customers cut their bills; trust keeps the next workload with us.

Stage 2 — Make Executive Decisions

Chief Technology Officer (CTO)

Research and development is $1,969.5 million — 42% of revenue. Recent launches include Snowpark Container Services, Snowflake Intelligence and Cortex Agents, Generation 2 warehouses and Snowflake Postgres; the company bought Crunchy Data and Observe. Open data formats let customers use compute “without requiring storage”. What does engineering build this round?

  • A.Build the new metered categories — transactions, AI agents, observability — so more kinds of work go on the same meter, within what the ceiling leaves after recapture and migration.
  • B.Improve price-performance on the core warehouse; it is the biggest part of consumption and the factor we name first.
  • C.Build the AI layer hardest, staffed through new equity grants, because AI agents are where new workloads are created.
  • D.Build for open formats and interoperability; being the best compute engine over open data keeps us in the deal.

Stage 2 — Key takeaways

Make efficiency support expansion

Pricing Strategy: Coordinate the product, selling effort and funding behind the meter.

  • Fund one complete plan. Recapture (40) + workload migration (25) + new metered categories (35) = 100 units, paid in cash. Free cash flow adds no extra units.
  • Keep improving the product. Pair the efficiency release with migration so freed customer budget returns as new workloads. Longer commitments alone do not establish consumption.
  • Compare the opportunity cost. Substituting the equity-funded AI layer makes the plan cost 110. The preferred plan broadens what the meter measures without changing its price level.

Simulation assumptions: planning units, the ceiling, the equity multiplier and option dependencies.

Stage 3 — CEO: Set Company Priorities

Chief Executive Officer (CEO)

As CEO, rank the four questions from most to least important for a company paid by the unit to still be growing in four years. Discuss your executives’ recommendations before setting the company’s priorities. Ask each executive: “What is your recommendation, which colleague’s constraint does it depend on, and why is your question important for the company to resolve?” You receive their answers through team chat, not automatic summaries.

  • Money — 29% growth against a $1.4 billion operating loss
  • Expansion — a retention rate falling from 133% to 125%
  • The paradox — every efficiency gain bills less
  • The roadmap — what else can go on the meter

Use each question once. Why should your first-ranked question take priority? Refer to information two executives shared, name the metric Snowflake charges for, say whether it still passes the test (does the number go up on its own when the customer succeeds?), and name one fence you would add.

Stage 3 — Key takeaways

Rank the constraints on growth

Pricing Strategy: Explain the metric, its limits and one proposed fence.

  • Put Paradox and Expansion first. Either order fits the template: efficiency lowers the bill for existing work, so bringing new workloads onto the platform is the immediate revenue problem.
  • Place Roadmap third and Money fourth. New categories enable expansion. The financial ceiling still binds the plan; ranking Money last does not make the budget optional.
  • Test the value metric. Consumption rises with customer success only when success creates more metered work. Use evidence from two executives and propose one fence, such as a minimum committed spend.

These priorities follow the template’s scenario assumptions; explain the dependencies behind your ranking.

Snowflake's Consumption Meter

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, November 5

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • As a group, name the corner of the triangle your decision moves — metric, level, or fences.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and defense.
💰

Financial Lead

Revenue +29% to $4,683.9 M at 67% gross margin. Operating loss $(1,435.2) M — 31% of revenue, improved from 40%. Free cash flow +$1,120.3 M. Stock-based compensation $1,599.5 M — 34% of revenue, with $3.1 B unvested to come. S&M is 44% of revenue; R&D 42%. What do you do?

  • A Drive stock-based compensation down as a share of revenue.
  • B Bring sales and marketing down further — the meter, not the sales team, expands accounts.
  • C Nothing structural — free cash flow is positive; the GAAP loss is how we pay people.
  • D Cut research and development — $1,969.5 M is the largest expense line.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • $1,599.5 M of stock-based compensation is larger than the entire operating loss — it is what explains the gap between a $1.4 B GAAP loss and $1.1 B of positive free cash flow.
  • "Non-cash" is not "free": $3.1 B of unvested awards is $3.1 B of future dilution — and shareholders pay it.
  • Cutting R&D trades the growth engine — the thing that creates new metered workloads — for a loss smaller than the stock-compensation line.
📣

Marketing Lead

Net revenue retention is 125%, from 126% and 133%. Customers 10,996 → 13,328. $1M+ customers 449 → 576 → 733. Global 2000 customers ≈ 43% of revenue. RPO $6,867.5 M → $9,771.5 M. S&M is $2,062.1 M (44% of revenue). Where does the effort go?

  • A Migrating new workloads inside existing customers.
  • B New logos — a wider base so no single expansion rate decides the year.
  • C The largest accounts — they have the most workloads left to move.
  • D Locking in longer capacity commitments; RPO is already $9,771.5 M.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Under consumption pricing, revenue grows when work moves onto the platform — and migrating new workloads inside existing customers is the direct answer to a retention rate falling from 133% to 125%.
  • RPO is not revenue: the filing states revenue is recognised on consumption, not ratably, and that deferred revenue "is not a meaningful indicator of future revenue." A commitment does not make anyone run a query.
  • 125% is still expansion — above 100 means existing customers spent more. The problem is the direction, not the level.
⚙️

Operation Lead

The filing warns efficiency lets customers "consume fewer… resources to accomplish the same workloads," that open formats let them use compute "without requiring storage," and that customers are "optimiz[ing] consumption." Gross margin held at 67%. What do you do?

  • A Ship the efficiency and go get the freed budget back as new workloads.
  • B Broaden what can be metered — transactions, agents, observability, applications.
  • C Help customers optimize deliberately and openly; price-performance is how we compete.
  • D Slow efficiency work on existing workloads; there's no reason to cut our own meter.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The filing's warning is conditional: revenue falls only "to the extent these improvements do not result in an offsetting increase in new workloads." The efficiency is not the problem; failing to convert it is.
  • Deliberately shipping a less efficient product while competing with AWS, Azure and GCP on price-performance is how you stop having the customer at all.
  • Open formats attack half the meter — compute without storage removes one of the three things being metered. That is a metric problem, not a level problem.
💻

Tech Lead

R&D is $1,969.5 M — 42% of revenue. Recent launches: Snowpark, Container Services, Snowflake Intelligence, Cortex Agents, Gen 2 Warehouses, Snowflake Postgres. Acquired Crunchy Data ($164.5 M) and Observe (~$596.2 M). Meanwhile open formats let customers use compute without storage. What does engineering build?

  • A New categories that put more kinds of work on the meter.
  • B The AI layer hardest — that's where new workloads are being created now.
  • C Open formats and interoperability — keep the compute even when we lose the storage.
  • D Price-performance on the core warehouse — the biggest part of consumption.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • A consumption model is only as large as the range of work it can measure — Postgres (transactions), Cortex Agents (AI) and Observe (observability) each widen the meter, which is why the company bought two of them.
  • Price-performance on the core warehouse is the paradox chosen deliberately, on the biggest possible base — efficiency destroys the most revenue with no new work created.
  • Open formats concede half the meter rather than widening it.
🔒

Security Lead

Under the shared-responsibility model, Snowflake secures the platform; customers configure their own controls. The filing concedes it "do[es] not have visibility into whether adequate access controls (such as MFA or network restrictions) are being enforced," and that customer failures have harmed the company "in the past." Where do you set the bar?

  • A Change the defaults so the secure configuration is what customers get without choosing it.
  • B Invest in visibility and proactive customer alerting.
  • C Invest in the platform and cloud infrastructure we are unambiguously responsible for.
  • D Make the shared-responsibility boundary clearer in contracts and documentation.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Shared responsibility is not shared reputation: the filing concedes customer failures have harmed the company, so the control that works is the one customers get without having to choose it.
  • You cannot enforce what you cannot see — but seeing a bad configuration is not the same as preventing one.
  • Contracts allocate liability; they do not allocate blame — whose name is in the headline?

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question.

After all five leads present their selection + one-sentence defense, rank the five decisions 1–5 by which the company must resolve first, allocate 100 points across them in blocks of 10, and name the metric: what does this company charge for, does that metric still pass the test — does the invoice go up on its own when the customer succeeds — and name one fence you would add?

🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Rank by what happens to the meter — the paradox and expansion belong at the top, and the rank-#1 sentence must contain a figure.
  • The metric is consumption: it still passes the success test (invoice grows when the customer succeeds) but fails the efficiency test (make the product better and the bill goes down).
  • RPO of $9.77 B does not make anyone consume anything — and a fixed 20/20/20/20/20 means the Account Manager decided nothing.

Key takeaway

Separate the three pricing decisions — metric, level, and fences. A pricing metric that punishes your own product improvements is a strategic contradiction: the invoice should rise when the customer succeeds, not when they wait.

Making the product better can make the revenue smaller.

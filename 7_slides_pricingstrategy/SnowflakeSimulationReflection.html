Snowflake's Consumption Meter

Simulation Reflection · Thursday, November 5

Group Discussion

  • Form groups with classmates who have the same role.
  • As a group, name the corner of the triangle your decision moves — metric, level, or fences.
  • Cite a figure from your packet, name what your choice gives up, and state one thing that would change your answer.
  • Select one representative to present your group's choice and reasoning.
💰

Financial Lead

Revenue grew 29%, the operating loss was $1.4 billion, and free cash flow was positive $1.1 billion. What do you do?

  • A Cut research and development. At $1,969.5 million it is 42% of revenue and the single largest expense line in the company.
  • B Do nothing structural. Free cash flow is $1,120.3 million and positive; the GAAP loss is an accounting artifact of how we pay people.
  • C Bring sales and marketing down further. It is 44% of revenue, already down from 50% two years ago, and under a consumption model the meter — not the sales team — is what expands an account.
  • D Drive stock-based compensation down as a share of revenue. At $1,599.5 million it is larger than the entire operating loss, and it is the reason a company with $1.1 billion of free cash flow still reports a $1.3 billion net loss.
💰

Financial Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Connect spending to the pricing model. Explain how the proposed allocation affects customer value, the cost to serve and the company's ability to capture revenue.
  • Distinguish the financial measures. Compare operating results, cash flow and compensation costs before drawing a conclusion about capacity to invest.
  • State the long-term tradeoff. Identify what the choice funds or sacrifices and what evidence would make you revisit it.
📣

Marketing Lead

Net revenue retention has fallen from 133% to 125%. Where does the selling effort go?

  • A Into locking in longer capacity commitments. Remaining performance obligations rose to $9,771.5 million — the more customers commit up front, the less any of this matters.
  • B Into new logos. Total customers went from 10,996 to 13,328, and a wider base is what stops any single expansion rate from deciding the year.
  • C Into migrating new workloads inside existing customers. Under a consumption metric, revenue grows when work moves onto the platform — and the $1 million-plus customer count going from 449 to 733 is what that looks like when it works.
  • D Into the largest accounts. 790 Forbes Global 2000 customers are already about 43% of revenue and they have the most workloads left to move.
📣

Marketing Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Expansion requires a behavioral explanation. State what customers would do differently under the proposed sales plan and how that affects the invoice.
  • Separate the measures. Explain what retention, customer counts and commitments reveal about demand, and what they do not establish.
  • Consider the fences. Identify which customer groups receive different value and how the proposal responds to those differences.
⚙️

Operation Lead

Every efficiency improvement you ship lets customers pay you less for the same work. What do you do about it?

  • A Slow down the efficiency work on existing workloads and direct engineering to features we can charge separately for. There is no reason to cut our own meter.
  • B Ship the efficiency and go get the freed budget back as new workloads — the filing itself says lower revenue follows only "to the extent these improvements do not result in an offsetting increase in new workloads."
  • C Help customers optimize deliberately and openly. Price-performance is a factor we say we compete on, and the customer who trusts our optimization advice keeps the next workload with us.
  • D Broaden what can be metered. Transactions, AI agents, observability and applications put more kinds of work on the same meter, so one workload getting cheaper matters less.
⚙️

Operation Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Efficiency changes the relationship between work and billing. Trace what happens to resources consumed, customer value and revenue under the proposed plan.
  • Test the response over time. Explain what customers might do with released capacity or budget and what evidence would support that expectation.
  • Locate the choice in the triangle. Identify whether the proposal changes the metric, level, fences or the work being delivered.

Tech Lead

R&D is $1.97 billion — 42% of revenue — and the revenue model is paid by the unit consumed. What does engineering build?

  • A Build price-performance improvements on the core warehouse. It is the biggest part of consumption and the competitive factor we name first.
  • B Build the AI layer hardest. Snowflake Intelligence and Cortex Agents are where new workloads are actually being created right now, and new workloads are what the revenue model needs.
  • C Build for open formats and interoperability. Customers are going there anyway, and being the best compute engine over open data keeps us in the transaction even when we lose the storage.
  • D Build the new categories that put more kinds of work on the meter — transactions, AI agents, observability — because a consumption model is only as big as the range of work it can measure.
💻

Tech Lead — Key Takeaways

  • The roadmap and the meter interact. Explain how the proposed capability changes customer value, measured usage and operating cost.
  • Compare alternatives from the customer's perspective. Identify the workflow each approach supports and the reasons a customer would choose it.
  • Define a test of the investment. State how you would assess adoption and revenue effects without treating every increase in activity as equal value.
🔒

Security Lead

Responsibility is shared. Blame is not. Where do you set the bar?

  • A Invest in visibility and proactive customer alerting — we cannot enforce controls we cannot see, so the first job is being able to see them.
  • B Invest in the platform and cloud infrastructure security we are unambiguously responsible for under the shared-responsibility model.
  • C Change the defaults so the secure configuration is what customers get without having to choose it — the filing admits we cannot see whether MFA is enforced, and that a customer's failure becomes our reputation.
  • D Make the shared-responsibility boundary clearer in contracts and documentation. Customer configuration is the customer's obligation and the agreement should say so plainly.
🔒

Security Lead — Key Takeaways

  • Trust affects willingness to use the service. Explain how the proposal could influence adoption, continued use and confidence in the platform.
  • Map responsibility in practice. Identify what the company and customer can configure, observe and correct, and where coordination is needed.
  • Assess the cost and effect of the control. Describe what would demonstrate that the proposal works and how it affects the customer experience.

Account Manager

You do not answer a functional question. After the five leads present, consider money, the efficiency paradox, trust, expansion, and the roadmap.

  • 1 Rank the problems 1–5 by what must be resolved to keep growing in five years. Use each once. Justify #1 in one sentence citing a figure from your packet.
  • 2 Allocate 100 points across the five problems in blocks of 10, totaling exactly 100.
  • 3 Name the metric. What does the company charge for? Does the invoice rise on its own when the customer succeeds? Propose one fence that lets customers receiving more value pay more without losing those receiving less.
🧭

Account Manager — Key Takeaways

  • Separate the three pricing decisions. Explain the metric, the level and the fences before assessing the company's performance.
  • Coordinate the five-year choices. Ranking sets order; weighting expresses relative emphasis. Show how the roadmap, expansion, efficiency, trust and spending decisions interact.
  • Test success and efficiency separately. Describe what happens to the invoice when a customer gains value and when it becomes more efficient. Use that reasoning to defend a proposed fence.

Key takeaway

Return to the coffee price: per what, at what level, and for which customers? Use the Value-Metric Triangle to explain how the team's choices affect customer behavior, delivered value and revenue.
